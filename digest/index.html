Auditable Human-Agent Teams for Autonomous X-ray Experimentation

arXiv:2611.12933v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Closed-loop autonomous experimentation can accelerate scientific discovery, but implementation effort limits accessibility. Agentic systems may lower this barrier, although sustained operation on physical instruments remains largely unexplored. Here, using coding-agent infrastructure grounded in validated scientific methods, we demonstrate autonomous synchrotron X-ray experiments on nanoparticle superlattices under evolving, human-defined objectives. Complete human-agent and agent-agent transcripts enable an audit that reconstructs decision provenance and characterizes the division of cognitive labor. Across three experiments, 61-67% of messages involved interpretation or judgment, while the share of methodological and scientific contributions increased from 46% in the setup-focused first experiment to 56-61% in later experiments. Provenance showed that 59-68% of error corrections involved multiple participants and that about half of all decisions incorporated the input of another participant. These results may guide design principles for multi-agent scientific systems and inform future agent harnesses, model training, and accessible autonomous experimentation.

EarthRecord: From Pixels to Land Change with Multimodal Agents over Four Decades of Satellite Imagery

arXiv:2611.12934v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: While multimodal agents now carry out complex data analysis with tools, their ability to turn decades of satellite imagery into reliable land-change analysis, crucial for Earth observation science, remains unmeasured. Existing benchmarks fall short: they pose questions on curated images or give agents preprocessed inputs, leaving out the raw multi-sensor archives where such analysis begins. We introduce EarthRecord, a benchmark built on the raw record of 56 sites: four decades of Landsat, MODIS, VIIRS, and Sentinel-2 data, with cloud masking and cross-sensor harmonization left to the agent. It poses 238 land-change questions across ten analysis tasks, scored against withheld references from global products, agreement among methods, and visual interpretation. Two controls test what answers rest on: the closed book removes the record, and instrument pairs alter one satellite's data while nothing changes on the ground. Evaluations of twelve multimodal agents reveal that leading agents complete most analyses end to end but falter when an answer requires mapping from imagery. Further analysis shows that the record moves answers from prior knowledge to the pixels but not yet to the real change on the ground: when one satellite's data are altered, half of the right answers follow the alteration. EarthRecord thus provides a principled platform for measuring and advancing agents from pixels to land change.

Train on prefill, monitor at decode: Using sparse autoencoders for topic monitoring

arXiv:2611.12935v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Topic monitoring is the task of labelling the topic of each token in a model's response during generation. Activation-based monitors that run at generation time have so far been trained to detect harmful content and are limited to two or three classes. We introduce TopicScope, a framework for training causal topic classifiers on SAE features that assign a topic to every token, so that the label can change partway through a response. We train three detector architectures on synthetic multi-topic conversations covering seven topics. Across Gemma-3 1B and 4B, monitors trained on features extracted from prefill hidden states were able to generalise to decoding. We also found that the classifiers are robust to perturbations, losing at most 5 macro-F1 points when off-topic asides are inserted, topics are removed or sections are reordered. Ablations conducted to understand the contribution of the SAE revealed that classifiers reaching baseline performance without it were several times larger. Our findings show that compact topic monitors can be trained inexpensively in prefill and deployed during generation. More broadly, our work shows where SAEs are useful beyond safety classification. Our code, dataset and checkpoints are available at https://github.com/Mercity-AI/SAE-Guardrails and https://huggingface.co/collections/Mercity/online-guardrails-using-sparse-autoencoders-6ac602940468c62fefb2b9e8

LandslideFM: A Foundation Model Towards Unified Landslide Perception and Understanding

arXiv:2611.12936v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Intelligent landslide detection is essential for disaster prevention. However, existing models primarily focus on isolated visual tasks, require extensive labeled data, and lack deep analytical and scene-understanding capabilities. To address these limitations, we propose LandslideFM, the first unified foundation model that connects visual perception tasks with contextual interpretation of landslides. Specifically, LandslideFM adapts to multiple visual tasks via a Source-Agreement Graduated Trust semi-supervised learning framework that leverages limited labeled data alongside abundant unlabeled data. Furthermore, predicted landslide regions provide explicit spatial guidance to a Vision-Language Model adapted through parameter-efficient fine-tuning, enabling comprehensive landslide reasoning. To support training and evaluation in this domain, we also present LandslideVL, the first benchmark for intelligent landslide analysis, combining visual imagery and newly introduced textual prompts. Comprehensive experiments across public datasets and LandslideVL demonstrate that LandslideFM achieves superior quantitative and qualitative performance. Most importantly, LandslideFM surpasses general-purpose VLMs in specialized landslide reasoning and scene analysis. Our code is released at https://github.com/wangshanwen001/LandslideFM.

An ensemble of graph generative models for assisted history matching of the Brugge field case

arXiv:2611.12927v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Assisted history matching remains non-unique because many geological realisations can reproduce equivalent production responses with substantial differences between individual well matches. This ambiguity increases when a field comprises several depositional settings and modelling strategies. The paper applied a graph autoencoder approach to history matching of a benchmark Brugge field that comprises different depositional zones. Each stratigraphic zone is modelled with its dedicated generative model that comprises a joint latent space for optimisation history matching loops. Graph-based generators operate directly on the active cells of the corner-point grid, so its irregular geometry is handled without conversion to a regular lattice. The optimisation search in the 160-dimensional joint latent space is done with the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy. The multi-component objective function includes static well data mismatch, dynamic production mismatch, and a geological realism penalty based on the Decoder induced latent metric. Latent space diagnostics with topological data analysis are applied prior to history matching to test whether one generator is sufficient or whether a multiple generator formulation is required. The results show that the zone-based formulation gives coherent latent spaces, preserves the main porosity and permeability relations that vary from one zone to another, and supports history matching while keeping the solutions within the learned geological manifold. The matched models reproduce field oil and water production within the prior uncertainty range and provide a comparable well-level match to previously published Brugge studies based on Ensemble Kalman Filter, Particle Swarm Optimisation with principal component analysis of parameterisation, and Multiple Kernel Learning with Particle Swarm Optimisation.

Exploration via Exploitation: The Blessing of Reward Diversity in Personalized Federated RL

arXiv:2611.12964v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: While federated reinforcement learning (RL) has traditionally guaranteed high efficiency in homogeneous environments, collaboration is often hindered when agents possess distinct goals. In this paper, we flip this paradigm, framing reward heterogeneity not as a hurdle to consensus, but as a structural asset that catalyzes exploration. We first propose Personalized Federated Upper Confidence Bound Value Iteration (PF-UCBVI), which achieves optimal linear speedup by decoupling shared dynamics from personalized objectives. To eliminate the risks of explicit exploration, we then introduce Personalized Federated Exploration-Free Value Iteration (PF-EFVI), a purely greedy algorithm that leverages reward diversity to ensure state-action coverage. We prove that PF-EFVI attains logarithmic regret without explicit exploration bonuses under sufficient reward diversity. Our results reveal that agent disagreement shifts the exploration burden from temporal complexity to spatial diversity, enabling efficient collective learning without explicit exploration.

StatPrompt-LLM: A Lightweight Statistical Refinement for Foundation-Style Time-Series Forecasting

arXiv:2611.12974v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Time-series forecasting has recently seen a paradigm shift with the introduction of foundation-style models that adapt large pretrained language architectures to sequential data. However, effectively exposing fundamental temporal structures, such as trend, scale, and temporal dependence, to these models remains an open challenge, particularly when the available observations are limited. In this paper, we conduct an exploratory empirical study across different generations of forecasting architectures and propose StatPrompt-LLM, a lightweight statistical refinement of Time-LLM. StatPrompt-LLM augments the existing Time-LLM prompting mechanism with dataset-level statistical information and introduces a zero-initialised autoregressive bypass as a complementary linear forecasting pathway. We evaluate our framework on electricity and meteorological time-series benchmarks, including ETTh1, ETTh2, and Weather, under full-prefix and reduced-prefix configurations. Under the 10% reduced-prefix configuration, StatPrompt-LLM achieved lower in-sample prediction errors than Time-LLM, with unweighted average relative reductions across datasets of 40.2% in MSE and 21.4% in MAE. Full-prefix results were more dataset-dependent. Ablation analyses revealed relatively modest and dataset-dependent changes when selected additional statistical descriptor groups were masked within the combined architecture. Computational profiling indicated additional model parameters and runtime overhead relative to Time-LLM.

Multimodal Graph Learning: A Survey of Models, Benchmarks, and Frontiers

arXiv:2611.12990v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multimodal attributed graphs (MAGs), in which nodes carry text, images, and other modalities while edges encode relations among entities, underlie e-commerce, social media, content platforms, and creative applications. Learning on MAGs has developed along two complementary lines: graph-centric methods redesign how modalities are fused, propagated, and connected within message passing, while foundation-model-centric methods pretrain transferable multimodal graph encoders or bring graph context into multimodal large language models for prediction and generation. In parallel, several benchmarks have appeared with different datasets and evaluation protocols. This survey takes an algorithm-centric and evaluation-grounded view of the field and makes three contributions. Modeling: we introduce a unified message-passing formulation and organize methods into five integration levels (feature, propagation, topology, learning, and foundation model), covering discriminative and graph-conditioned generative models; within this formulation we derive representative methods as special cases, compare their assumptions, expressiveness, and cost, and identify cross-modal sensitivity as a property that separates otherwise similar architectures. Datasets and evaluation: we review recent benchmarks, summarize their datasets and metrics, define model-independent descriptors (label homophily, average degree, clustering, and edge provenance), and map each evaluation protocol to the baselines it uses. Frontiers: we connect multimodal graph learning to AI agents, covering the multimodal graphs that agents perceive and remember and graph engineering, the practice of organizing agent workflows as explicit graphs; we then discuss open problems, concrete efforts for the community, and research directions, including when graph structure helps in the presence of strong multimodal foundation models.
