Formalizing inverse problems in the age of AI: possibilities, pitfalls, and an invitation

arXiv:2611.06962v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Proof assistants such as Lean check mathematical proofs down to the axioms, and AI agents have recently made writing such proofs cheap. We describe AutoIP, a Lean library in which classical linear regularization theory, together with the functional analysis it rests on, has been autoformalized by AI agents. A machine-checked proof certifies only the formal statement it proves, so the remaining human task is to confirm that each formal statement says what was intended. We organize the library around this task: a reviewer compares the formal statements with their natural-language counterparts and never needs to read a proof. Our experience is that the risks lie in the statements rather than in the proofs, and that simple automated tests on the statements can expose problems even in the established literature. We illustrate this with a textbook theorem whose hypothesis is more restrictive than asserted. We invite the inverse problems community to extend and build on the library.

KDASO: Knowledge-Data Dual-Driven Automated Skill Optimization for Liability Adjudication Tasks

arXiv:2611.06972v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Liability adjudication tasks (e.g., legal charge determination) require analyzing event descriptions against domain-specific skills to determine whether a party should bear liability. While Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown promise in this area by leveraging executable skills for interpretable reasoning, existing approaches face three critical limitations: skill libraries become stale as real-world scenarios dynamically evolve, skill descriptions often suffer from a semantic execution gap where LLMs deviate from intended expert logic, and growing skill collections exceed context budgets causing critical skill omission. To address these challenges, we propose Knowledge-Data Dual-Driven Automated Skill Optimization (KDASO), which continuously evolves a skill library through neighborhood-conditioned induction from recent adjudication data, execution-aligned refinement based on LLM trajectory analysis, and context-budgeted routing for scalable skill selection. Experiments across ride-hailing adjudication, legal liability determination, and logical reasoning judgment demonstrate that KDASO significantly outperforms current general-purpose and domain-specific baselines. KDASO further achieves 8.96% improvement when transferring skills to concept-similar scenarios and maintains consistent performance gains through continuous optimization, underscoring the high transferability and stability of generated skills.

BlockLock: Reproducible LLM Inference with Fixed Batch Geometry

arXiv:2611.06977v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Reproducible LLM inference requires that a request produces the same output regardless of the other requests in the batch. Existing solutions pay for this in different ways: batch-invariant kernels constrain every operator, and isolating prefill with fixed-shape decode ({our CoRun-style baseline}) gives up shared prefill compute and pads decode. We present BlockLock, which keeps shared batched prefill and pins only the numerical path of the protected request: it fixes the geometry that the request sees, with {two modes: BlockLock-Rows uses fixed-width rows and retains batched decode of all requests; BlockLock-Pack uses terminal-anchored packing with an isolated dummy segment, then extracts the protected request's KV state for target-only decode. Under a stated per-deployment contract, both modes preserve the target's output bitwise when its companions change.} Across five {dense} models (Qwen3-8B/14B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Gemma-3-4B/12B-IT) and three single-GPU deployments (NVIDIA A6000, A100, and H100), we evaluate our approach on both tinyBenchmarks and BFCL{; we also include initial tensor-parallel experiments on two NVIDIA A6000 GPUs. Both modes preserve target sequences and Top20-logit bits in the reported tests.} At equal delivered work, {BlockLock-Rows} reduces latency by 53.5--55.0\% and improves throughput by 115.2--122.2\% relative to vLLM Batch-Invariant; compared with batch-invariant kernels, it needs no custom kernel implementations and constrains only the protected requests.

Resource-Efficient Reinforcement Learning Post-Training through Elastic Expert Parallelism

arXiv:2611.06984v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Reinforcement learning post-training improves the capabilities of large language models, but generating training responses is a major computational bottleneck. In mixture-of-experts models, workers that finish their assigned responses may remain occupied because their experts are still needed by workers processing longer responses. Consequently, approximately one-third of occupied worker time in the baseline occurs after local response generation finishes. Here we present FlexFloor, a system that enables earlier resource release during synchronous response generation while preserving the expert computation required by ongoing responses. FlexFloor jointly adapts response scheduling and the active group of workers. It uses historical response lengths and memory constraints to guide batching and prompt placement, then progressively releases workers while maintaining expert availability and correct communication. Compared with the verl baseline, FlexFloor generates approximately 65.40% more response tokens per unit of occupied computing time and increases response generation throughput by 52.74%, with little observed variation in rollout reward. By making reinforcement learning post-training more efficient, FlexFloor supports faster development and iterative improvement of large language models and AI agents.

SteelAgent: LLM-Orchestrated, Physics-Informed Steel Property Prediction with Audited Generalization and Calibrated Uncertainty

arXiv:2611.06953v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Steel property models can perform well on random test splits even when they extrapolate poorly to unfamiliar alloys. SteelAgent couples metallurgical descriptors and machine-learning models with a language-model interface for property prediction, uncertainty reporting and inverse design. We examine how the choice of held-out unit changes the conclusions drawn from SteelBench tensile-strength evaluations. For gradient boosting, pooled out-of-fold $R^2$ fell from 0.859 on a random split to 0.113 when steel families were held out. Octopus: One Brain, Many Branches for Latent-KV Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

arXiv:2611.06956v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multi-agent LLM systems explore in parallel by spawning sub-agents with isolated context windows, whereas single-agent systems append every search and verification step into one growing trajectory. However, existing multi-agent frameworks duplicate prefix KV caches across branches, exchange findings by re-prefilling text summaries, and flatten or drop cross-branch dependencies during reinforcement learning (RL). We introduce Octopus, a single-model multi-agent RL framework in which all threads share one backbone and coordinate over a GPU-resident directed acyclic graph (DAG) of KV states through five primitives (fork, read_thread, wait_threads, rewind, commit). Octopus combines three co-designed mechanisms: (1) Zero-Copy Radix Forking, which spawns branches as $\mathcal{O}(1)$ cursor views over shared physical KV pages; (2) Attention-Only KV Mounting, which mounts a sibling thread's terminal KV blocks directly into full-attention layers while bypassing linear-recurrent states (Gated DeltaNet / Mamba-2), cutting off-task hijacking by $21\times$ ($57.9\% \to 2.7\%$) while retaining $92.2\%$ of full-text mutual information at zero extra KV allocation; and (3) a Packed DAG-GRPO Trainer, which packs all episode branches into one block-sparse attention pass and assigns counterfactual branch credit via leave-one-branch-out marginal rewards. Trained end-to-end with GRPO on Qwen3.5-4B over a sparse BM25 corpus, Octopus reaches $49.6\%$ accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus ($+17.5$ points over single-agent GRPO at 143K context and $+12.5$ points over text-summary multi-agent RL) and $51.2\%$ on OpenResearcher, outperforming dense-retrieval agents up to $250\times$ larger while increasing training throughput $3.3\times$ and cutting peak KV memory $2.8\times$.

Uncertainty calibration in Bayesian M/EEG source imaging

arXiv:2611.06979v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: M/EEG source imaging requires accurate localization and reliable uncertainty estimates, yet posterior credible regions are rarely assessed for empirical calibration. We present a simulation-based framework for uncertainty quantification and calibration of empirical Bayesian inverse methods with closed-form conditional Gaussian posterior summaries. We compare Bayesian minimum norm (BMN) with sLORETA depth weighting and sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) with sparse basis field expansions (S-FLEX). S-FLEX allows retained coefficients to contribute nonzero marginal posterior variance at locations where voxel-wise SBL yields degenerate variances. For BMN, we derive joint source- and noise-variance updates through evidence maximization. We test whether adaptive noise learning yields near-calibrated uncertainties. Both methods retain residual miscalibration, motivating post-hoc isotonic recalibration of credible-region levels using empirical coverage. Simulations assess fixed- and free-orientation models across signal-to-noise ratios (SNR), source sparsity levels, and subject-specific head models, using oracle, baseline-estimated, and adaptively learned noise. Before recalibration, SBL+S-FLEX yields lower Wasserstein localization error, smaller posterior uncertainty, and coverage closer to nominal credibility than BMN+sLORETA. Isotonic recalibration improves both methods, with larger gains for conservative BMN+sLORETA posteriors; transfer across mismatched SNR, sparsity, and head models remains condition dependent. Noise specifications yield broadly consistent reconstruction and uncertainty trends within each estimator. In real auditory MEG data, SBL+S-FLEX provides more focal localization and tighter uncertainties than BMN+sLORETA, suggesting greater statistical power for identifying bilateral auditory sources.

Robust Stream Classification using Time Neutralising Decision Trees

arXiv:2611.06991v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Data Streams are characterised as a potentially infinite source of data subject to non-stationarity. This non-stationarity, also called Concept Drift, often results in deployed predictive models having to be perpetually retrained as the underlying data generating process of the stream changes over time. Drift Detectors are a class of methods used to identify these changes, triggering these retraining procedures. Historically, Stream Learning has operated under the assumption that instances are only seen once, and cannot be stored for later use. Contemporary work relaxes this assumption, yet drift detection and simple resetting procedures are still ubiquitous. Building on related work which has shown that traditional Batch Learning algorithms often outperform Stream Learning algorithms when instances can be stored, we introduce Time Neutralising Trees (TNT), a Decision Tree architecture that enables robust stream classification in settings with Concept Drift. During training, TNT will filter out ("neutralise") old training samples that are no longer relevant given the current context of the stream. If those instances become relevant again, TNT will then re-include them. We evaluate TNT, and related algorithms on 24 real and semi-real classification data streams from the USP DS repository. Results show strong evidence that TNT achieves best-in-class performance on a wide-range of data streams with varying concept drift types.

Where heat flows and where to change it: gauge-free sensitivity maps of thermal conductivity in disordered solids

arXiv:2611.06958v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Designing materials for thermal management requires knowing where heat flows, not only how much. Within harmonic (Allen$--$Feldman) theory we develop two local descriptions and test them on amorphous silicon. The first, a projection of the conductivity $\kappa$ onto atoms, depends on how the potential energy is divided among atoms (a "gauge"); we find the dependence moderate, 23% of the map's spatial standard deviation at single sites and 9.5% over regions of radius 0.4 nm, apart from a uniform offset of 0.8% of $\kappa$. The second avoids the division altogether. It is the exact response of $\kappa$ to the mass of each atom and the stiffness of each bond, it obeys an Euler sum rule, and it contains the heat-flow map as one of three exact terms. In amorphous and paracrystalline silicon, with ACE, GAP-18 and Keating force constants, the two maps are anti-correlated: where little heat flows a heavier atom lowers $\kappa$, and where much heat flows it tends to raise it. The explanation is local softness. The harmonic vibrational amplitude of each atom predicts both maps, and at room temperature the anti-correlation largely disappears at fixed amplitude, in a 32,768-atom replica as well; below about 100 K a component remains that softness does not capture. Thus soft regions, which carry little heat, act through the mixing of vibrational modes about 1$--$2 meV apart. In paracrystalline silicon the grains conduct more heat per atom, while the disordered matrix controls the response. Exact recalculations show that the response map guides the placement of dispersed substituents and locates the region that limits transport across an amorphous/crystalline interface.
