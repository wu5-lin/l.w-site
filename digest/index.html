Experimental realization of twofold topological phase transition induced by long-range hoppings in one-dimensional chains

arXiv:2611.08605v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Long-range hoppings offer a powerful mechanism to engineer exotic topological phases beyond conventional tight-binding models. Here, we experimentally demonstrate a twofold topological phase transition driven by third-nearest-neighbor couplings in a one-dimensional phononic chain that emulates cis-polyacetylene. Tunable hopping parameters in highly structured aluminum plates allow us to observe the emergence of Dirac points, a valley pseudospin structure, and topological edge states. The experimental results are supported by non-equilibrium Green's function transport calculations. Our findings confirm the predicted long-range phase diagram and establish phononic metamaterials as a versatile platform for exploring topological phenomena beyond the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger paradigm.

Jackknife-conformal uncertainty for linear ACE potentials: calibrated forces and silicon properties compared with Bayesian and POPS baselines

arXiv:2611.08678v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Machine-learned interatomic potentials are misspecified, so Bayesian posterior variances understate their errors. For linear atomic cluster expansion (ACE) potentials we derive calibrated uncertainties from one misspecification-robust parameter covariance, the delete-one-configuration jackknife. Scaled by one conformal quantile from a held-out part of the training set, it gives per-atom force uncertainties close to their target coverage on held-out silicon data and on cracks left out of the fit; screw dislocations left out are under-covered by the smaller of the two models. With one scale fitted to the forces of a held-out test split, the same parameter distribution is propagated to 21 properties of silicon, from elastic constants to defect and surface energies, by implicit differentiation through each relaxation and by committees, and is tested against density functional theory (DFT) without tuning on any property. For a smaller and a larger model the intervals cover most properties, and the larger model's are about 4 times tighter. The Bayesian posterior with one calibrated scale has the wrong shape: it covers fewer properties and has far lower joint evidence. Pointwise optimal parameter sets (POPS), once their hypercube is numerically defined, performs comparably on the properties, but its per-atom force regions are not calibrated to a target (0.95 and 0.84 at a nominal 0.90). Where the jackknife fails is relaxed point defects, whose training cells are all hot snapshots: a bias that every configuration shares is invisible to a jackknife, but it can be measured from the training labels, and as a discrepancy term, devised after the property results were seen, it brings the coverage close to nominal.

Ion Redistribution and Lattice-Distortion-Modulated d-Electron Correlation in Layered Multiferroic ABP$_2$S$_6$ Probed by X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy

arXiv:2611.08728v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Ferroelectric phase transitions are driven by ion displacement-induced symmetry breaking, yet their chemical signatures are rarely probed directly at the atomic level. Here, we establish temperature-dependent X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) as a powerful and quantitative probe of ferroelectric phase transitions by tracking ion migration-induced changes in local chemical environments. Using van der Waals (vdW) layered ABP$_2$S$_6$ materials as a model system, we systematically compare CuCrP$_2$S$_6$ (CCPS), which undergoes a ferroelectric phase transition, with AgCrP$_2$S$_6$ (ACPS), which remains paraelectric over the same temperature range. While ACPS exhibits only smooth trivial thermal broadening and shifts in core-level spectra, CCPS shows pronounced and enormously large temperature-dependent XPS anomalies across the ferroelectric transition, demonstrating that XPS is intrinsically sensitive to ferroelectric order. Detailed analysis of the Cu 2p spectra reveals that these anomalies originate from temperature-driven Cu$^+$ ion migration associated with polarization switching, whereas concurrent changes in the Cr core levels reflect secondary lattice squeezing and modulation of d-electron correlation induced by Cu displacement. Supported by a ferroelectric structural model, we further correlate XPS binding-energy shifts with the magnitude of Cu-ion migration, enabling a quantitative estimation of ionic displacement directly from spectroscopic observables. This work also highlights the rich information provided by temperature-dependent XPS for detecting and quantifying ferroelectric phase transitions and ion migration, offering a general chemical probe for ferroic materials and ion-displacement-driven phase transitions.

Bichromatic light-induced electronic torques on the N\'eel vector in Rashba magnetic multipoles from $p$-wave magnets to $i$-wave altermagnets

arXiv:2611.08761v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Light can control magnetic order on ultrafast timescales, but for a two-dimensional magnet with an out-of-plane order parameter $\boldsymbol N\parallel\hat z$, the inverse Faraday field is collinear with $\boldsymbol N$ and gives no transverse torque. Here we show that bichromatic $(\omega,b\omega)$ light generates electronic torques perpendicular to $\boldsymbol N$ in Rashba-coupled magnetic multipoles, from $p$-wave ($n=1$) magnets to $i$-wave ($n=6$) altermagnets. Beyond high-frequency effective fields, we evaluate the exact Floquet response and the generalized torque per occupied electron, for prescribed occupations. The momentum-dependent exchange terms dominate this Fermi-sea torque and remove the leading-order zeros (amplitude-ratio blindspots) of the momentum-independent field in the channels tested. For out-of-plane $\boldsymbol N$, rotational symmetry permits a transverse torque only if $\gcd(n,b+1)=1$ for counter-rotating beams or $\gcd(n,b-1)=1$ for co-rotating beams. For in-plane $\boldsymbol N$, the Fermi sea also gives a torque component along $\hat z$ that the momentum-independent field cannot produce. We classify the active channels, with $n=1,\dots,6$ and $b=1,\dots,7$, by frequency scaling and origin (cycle-averaged versus oscillating exchange). As a control, a Rashba ferromagnet ($n=0$) has one symmetry-allowed out-of-plane channel and a weak in-plane response without a selection rule. Finite-momentum occupied-state physics is therefore essential for light-driven torques in unconventional magnets, and the exact Floquet calculation validates and extends the high-frequency description.

Absolute Calibration of a Broadband Transmission Grating Spectrometer from 5 to 400 nm with Uncertainty Estimates

arXiv:2611.09288v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present the absolute calibration of a transmission grating spectrometer (TGS) with uncertainty estimates in the 5--400 nm range using synchrotron radiation at the BEAR beamline at Elettra light source in Trieste, Italy. Firstly, the spectral responsivity of a Si photodiode in the spectrometer was absolutely calibrated with respect to the absolutely-calibrated beamline photodiode. Subsequently, this photodiode was used to calibrate the transmittance of Zr and Al thin-film filters, along with bulk MgF$_2$ and UV-grade fused silica windows at their operating positions in the spectrometer to minimize mounting-induced uncertainties. Next, the quantum efficiency of the spectrometer's CCD camera was calibrated over the 5--400 nm range. Lastly, three gratings with line densities of 10000, 3000, and 1000 lines/mm were calibrated for spectroscopy in the ranges extending from soft X-ray to extreme-, vacuum- and deep-ultraviolet (DUV) wavelengths, respectively. The combination of various filters and gratings enables absolutely calibrated spectroscopy with high versatility across a broad wavelength range from soft X-rays to DUV.

Spin to Orbital Angular Momentum Conversion in the Neutron Nucleus Weak Interaction

arXiv:2611.09677v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The neutron nucleus weak interaction generates a small parity non conserving rotation of the neutron spin as the neutron moves through matter. This parity-odd neutron rotary power can reach up to 2.2e-3 rad * cm-1 for slow neutrons in 139La, which possesses a p-wave resonance at 0.7 eV where the parity-odd asymmetry is greatly amplified. Scanning Probe Control, Quantum Dynamics, and Dispersive Readout of Suspended Carbon Nanotube Resonators

arXiv:2611.09581v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Suspended carbon nanotubes (CNTs) offer promising opportunities for quantum nanomechanics owing to their small effective mass, strong electrostatic tunability, and mechanical nonlinearity. We develop a unified theoretical framework for coherent control and readout of a suspended CNT driven by an atomic force microscope (AFM) tip and capacitively coupled to a Cooper-pair box (CPB). We first derive the linear and nonlinear Duffing response of the CNT and quantify the effects of AFM force gradients, tip-position fluctuations, and voltage noise on mechanical stability, dephasing, and heating. These results establish operating conditions for coherent actuation with limited backaction. We then quantize the Duffing oscillator, derive its Kerr anharmonicity, and identify the requirements for selective control of individual vibrational transitions. Estimates of thermal occupation and active cooling illustrate the constraints for CNT modes spanning frequencies from 54 MHz to 5.37 GHz. Finally, we derive the CNT--CPB interaction and its dispersive limit, showing how phonon number and parity can be mapped onto the CPB through Ramsey interferometry and detected using quantum-capacitance readout. The resulting framework identifies the conditions under which localized AFM actuation, nonlinear quantum dynamics, and superconducting state-resolved detection can operate within a single CNT device.
