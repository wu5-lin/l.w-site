A Small Model Asks Better: Fine-Tuning Query Clarification for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

arXiv:2610.28686v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Query clarification is essential for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems that face ambiguous user queries. We consider multi-turn grounded conversational scenarios where a user query is either ill-formed or ambiguous in the context of the prior dialog or, more interestingly, it may be well-formed but when posed against retrieved documents there are multiple relevant interpretations. The task is to recognize both such cases and then generate an appropriate clarification request to the user. We present ClariEdge, a new dataset of approximately 3,900 examples that include such ambiguous queries along with negative examples that do not require clarification. We also show that a lightweight fine-tuned LoRA achieves 96.6% classification accuracy and outperforms prompted baselines of many larger models on both classification and quality of clarification generation. Our study shows that hard negatives are an essential ingredient to separate out queries that are truly ambiguous from queries that may look ambiguous but are not (i.e., can be resolved from the context). The same LoRA achieves high performance in both pre-retrieval and post-retrieval scenarios; thus, it can be used in two places in a RAG pipeline as a check before answer generation. The ClariEdge dataset is available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/ibm-research/ClariEdge.

Scaling in Games: Continued Pre-Training For Embodied Agents In Diverse Virtual Worlds

arXiv:2610.28698v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Scaling data, diversity and model capacity has improved embodied learning in physical environments, where diverse experiences remain grounded in shared physical laws. Whether these gains extend to virtual worlds with different visual styles, dynamics, controls, and objectives remains unclear. We investigate this question through a controlled study of continued pre-training (CPT) across three axes: data volume, diversity, and model size. Our 120 training configurations span four model sizes from 0.8B to 27B, six data budgets from 50 to 1,600 hours, and five mixtures ranging from Minecraft-only data to diverse multi-world corpora. We assess performance and generalization using three-level validation losses and gameplay benchmarks from in-corpus worlds to unseen worlds. Our results show: (1) more data improves in-corpus performance, diversity improves transfer to related worlds with limited gains in distant worlds, and model size improves data efficiency but generalizes worse to distant worlds under single-world training; (2) we observe an early interface phase with broad cross-world benefits and a later world phase with gains narrowed to in-corpus and related worlds, and fit an empirical scaling law relating validation loss to data volume, diversity and model size; and (3) our models are competitive with Minecraft specialists and frontier models in 3D worlds, and perform best in real-time settings. However, the limits of imitation and persistent gaps in distant worlds suggest that building generalist embodied agents requires richer training stages beyond continued pre-training.

SpecEvo: Speculative Evolution with Large Language Models for Cost-Efficient Scientific Discovery

arXiv:2610.28706v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLM-driven evolutionary search is a promising paradigm for scientific discovery, but existing methods remain costly and slow, often applying expensive reasoning uniformly while underusing evidence from failed, redundant, and saturated trials. We introduce Speculative Evolution (SpecEvo), a multi-LLM framework that coordinates lightweight and frontier models through continual consolidation of search evidence. Lightweight models power both parallel Speculators, which generate and refine candidate programs, and an Advisor, which distills accumulated successes, failures, and saturated directions into persistent guidance for subsequent exploration. At sparse checkpoints, a frontier-model Navigator inspects the evolving population and intervenes through synthesis, surgical refinement, or reframing according to search stagnation. This speculate-then-consolidate workflow delegates routine exploration and evidence consolidation to inexpensive models while reserving frontier reasoning for strategic interventions whose benefits extend across many subsequent low-cost evaluations. Across 176 tasks spanning four scientific and algorithmic discovery domains, SpecEvo matches or outperforms frontier-model baselines while reducing API cost by up to 80%, achieves up to 1.5x greater evaluation efficiency than cost-efficient baselines on equation discovery, and improves over the strongest CO-Bench baseline by 3.8%. These results support trajectory-conditioned model coordination as an effective approach to cost-efficient scientific discovery.

Automating ML for Science: Can Frontier Agents Climb Scientific Hills in the Wild?

arXiv:2610.28763v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Frontier coding agents are increasingly framed as end-to-end automated researchers, and ML for science offers a demanding test of that promise: improving a scientific model often requires domain insight beyond routine ML engineering. We introduce Agent4Sci, a long-horizon testbed for measuring how far today's agents can improve the published best models on scientific ML benchmarks, spanning 20 benchmarks across 11 domains. Rather than ranking agents on a leaderboard, Agent4Sci asks what kinds of progress agents can make on these tasks under in-the-wild conditions, and where they fall short. Empirically, today's frontier agents advance the published best on every benchmark, with Claude Opus reaching 10.4% mean relative improvement and no run flagged for academic misconduct. These gains, however, come almost entirely from a bag of ML-engineering tricks rather than research-level insight. Trajectory analysis surfaces three recurring limitations in this setting: novelty miscalibration, resource under-utilization, and rapid collapse into local engineering sweeps. Lightweight plug-in skills for research ideation, experiment management, and scheduled checklists raise Claude Opus from 10.4% to 20.1% and Codex from 4.4% to 8.1%, while yielding more diverse, domain-relevant interventions. Agent4Sci gives the community a way to track and push frontier agents from ML engineering toward domain insight on real-world ML-for-science problems. Code and data are available on GitHub (https://github.com/maszhongming/agent4sci) and Hugging Face (https://huggingface.co/datasets/MingZhong/agent4sci).

MoEZip: Routing-Aware KV Cache Compression for Sparse Mixture-of-Experts LLMs

arXiv:2610.30206v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models scale model capacity efficiently by activating a small subset of parameters per token. However, in long-context inference, memory remains a major bottleneck because the KV cache grows linearly with sequence length. Existing KV cache compression methods reduce this cost for dense transformers, but overlook a problem specific to MoE: KV eviction can change which experts are selected during answer generation, degrading the generation quality. We propose MoEZip, a routing-aware KV cache compression for sparse MoE language models. To estimate the sensitivity of expert routing to perturbation, we propose a novel metric based on the Fisher information matrix of routing distributions. MoEZip combines this metric with attention weights for routing-aware scoring of contexts. Finally, we compose a complementary scoring system that combines eviction methods for dense and sparse-MoE architectures. Experiments show that MoEZip achieves robust performance under aggressive cache compression on various benchmarks. At a KV retention ratio of 10\%, MoEZip improves over the SoTA baseline by +24 and +20 points on average on LongBench and RULER, corresponding to 2.3$\times$ and 4.9$\times$ higher scores, respectively.

Spectral Uncertainty Decomposition for Sparse Bayesian Learning

arXiv:2610.30207v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Estimating distributed neural sources from M/EEG measurements is an ill-posed inverse problem where principled uncertainty quantification remains challenging. The usual conditional Gaussian posterior covariance does not explicitly distinguish uncertainty along likelihood-visible source directions from uncertainty that is invisible to the measurements. We propose a spectral framework based on the singular value structure of the prior-preconditioned forward operator, which provides an exact, interpretable decomposition of the posterior covariance into data-informed and indeterminacy epistemic components. This formulation identifies uncertainty contributions that are reducible by better measurements from those that are fundamentally irreducible by sensing geometry. We further derive an equivalent subtractive decomposition that expresses the prior covariance as the sum of posterior and data-driven reduction terms, enabling precise accounting of uncertainty reduction through Bayesian learning. Together, these complementary representations offer both theoretical insight and practical tools for interpretable uncertainty quantification in M/EEG source imaging for linear-Gaussian Bayesian inverse solvers where the posterior covariance is available in closed form.

Prediction Is Not Calibration: Item Parameter Recovery and Representation Coupling in Knowledge Tracing

arXiv:2610.30211v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Knowledge tracing (KT) models can estimate item parameters through an item response decoder trained solely for next-response prediction, yet predictive accuracy does not guarantee parameter recovery. We study 2PL, GPCM, and NRM decoders paired with LSTM, Transformer, and DKVMN encoders. Response-model derivatives show why discrimination and option slope updates depend directly on the scale and spread of estimated ability. Affine logit identities show how item-dependent shifts and rescaling in estimated ability are absorbed into item parameters without changing response probabilities. In simulations, difficulty, mean threshold, and correct-option intercept recovery remain high under shuffled order across representation widths, whereas discrimination and keyed option slope contrast recovery require wider item representations and degrade further under fixed item order. Separating the sequence item representation (width $d_S$) from the item parameter key (width $d_M$), and pairing a wide key with a thin sequence representation ($\mathrm{SK}(64,8)$), reduces fixed-order ability distortions and improves recovery and cross-order consistency without sacrificing test accuracy or increasing model size relative to a shared width-64 baseline ($\mathrm{SH}(64)$). On TIMSS and EdNet, widening $d_M$ consistently improves agreement with an offline IRT reference, while the trade-off from narrowing $d_S$ depends on the encoder's reliance on sequence width for prediction. These findings show why item parameters, especially discrimination and option slopes, must be evaluated separately from response prediction.

AI Mindmeld: A Latent Interconnect Protocol

arXiv:2610.30215v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLMs can act as scientific agents using tools to consult specialists and run experiments. Yet specialist information must enter their context, generally as text, images, audio or video: a bottleneck between models with rich, high-dimensional representations. We advocate direct representation exchange: a mindmeld conveying many properties, including information an LLM would not think to request. It should be extensible to new specialists, flexible across questions and tasks, and compositional across independently connected specialists without joint training. The Latent Interconnect Protocol (LIP) learns connectors mapping specialist representations into LLM internal states. Training only connectors keeps this practical as LLMs grow and evolve. We connect three protein and three small molecule specialists to Gemma 3 (4B and 12B parameters) and Kimi K2.6 (1T total, 32B active), freezing all pretrained models. Independently trained connectors provide useful information without changing the LLM or existing connectors, supporting familiar and new question types about unseen objects. We demonstrate zero-shot transfer to cross-domain tasks without joint training. Task performance and compositionality generally improve with LLM capability, with especially strong gains in zero-shot task transfer. Limitations remain: current LLMs were not trained for specialist representations as native inputs; access does not ensure reliable interpretation, integration or use in reasoning. We call on specialist developers to provide consistent representation interfaces and, especially, on LLM developers to train models to receive, combine and reason with these inputs. These efforts could expand scientific agents' information while preserving conventional tools' flexibility. Data, pretrained connectors, and code for training, evaluation and interactive inference: https://github.com/PolymathicAI/AI-Mindmeld

Electronic Screening as a Design Lever in La-Doped BaTiO$_3$: Coupled Polarization, Dielectric Response, and Optical Anisotropy

arXiv:2610.30210v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Donor substitution can tune the coupled ferroelectric, dielectric, electronic, and optical properties of perovskite oxides by modifying lattice distortions and electronic screening. We investigate these effects in La-doped BaTiO3 using density functional theory, density-functional perturbation theory, and the Berry-phase formalism for polarization. For a model with 12.5% La substitution at the Ba site, La incorporation induces anisotropic lattice contraction, reduces tetragonality and Ti off-centering, and weakens the energetic stabilization of the tetragonal ferroelectric phase. The accompanying reduction in polarization is associated with enhanced electronic screening and reduced anomalous Born effective charges. Phonon calculations indicate that the relaxed doped structure is dynamically stable, while hardening of a low-frequency polar mode is consistent with weakened ferroelectricity. The high-frequency dielectric response increases moderately, whereas the static dielectric response decreases as the lattice contribution is suppressed. Electronic-structure calculations show a Fermi-level shift toward the conduction band without mid-gap defect states. The optical spectra exhibit an absorption-edge shift consistent with a Burstein-Moss-type mechanism, enhanced near-edge transparency, and reduced birefringence. Together, these results suggest a microscopic connection between donor-induced screening, polar-mode hardening, reduced dynamical charges, polarization suppression, and decreased optical anisotropy. The conclusions apply to the investigated 12.5% composition; calculations at additional concentrations are needed to establish quantitative doping trends.

Domain walls in $R3c$-structured NaNbO$_3$

arXiv:2610.30318v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In this work, we undertake a systematic first-principles study of structural domain walls in the low-temperature rhombohedral $R3c$ phase of perovskite oxide NaNbO$_3$. Employing a group-theoretic classification, we introduce eight unique classes of domain walls in the $R3c$ structure, four of which have a domain wall orientation determined uniquely by the conditions of elastic compatibility and charge neutrality, and perform structural relaxations for each representative domain wall class. We find that one class of 109{\deg} domain walls has a notably low energy of 9 mJ m$^{-2}$, comparable to the low-energy ferroelectric walls in BaTiO$_3$. Finally, we find that this class of 109{\deg} walls is compatible with the crystal twinning observed in experiments on the $R3c$ phase, and propose an atomistic mechanism for the $R3c \leftrightarrow Pbcm$ phase transition.

Peridynamic Thermo-Mechanical Modeling of Cracking in Tungsten under Fusion-Relevant Heat Fluxes

arXiv:2610.30356v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Plasma-facing materials (PFMs) are the primary armor materials in fusion reactors, protecting reactor wall components from extreme heat fluxes, energetic particles, and intense neutron irradiation. These harsh conditions induce thermo-mechanical stresses, irradiation damage, and overall degradation in PFMs. Among these, thermo-mechanical damage induced by extreme heat flux is one of the key factors limiting the lifetime of PFMs. We present a modeling and simulation approach for predicting thermo-mechanical cracking and damage in tungsten, a leading PFM candidate, under fusion-reactor-relevant heat fluxes. Specifically, we study damage induced by cyclic electron beam (e-beam) exposure under different combinations of base temperature and heat flux. We carry out component-scale thermo-mechanical simulations using CabanaPD, a peridynamics fracture mechanics code, informed by experimental data for recrystallized pure tungsten from the JUDITH e-beam facility. The simulations compare well with the experimental classification and enable identification of cracking thresholds across a range of heat fluxes and base temperatures. These predictions can provide detailed insights into the spatial distribution and temporal accumulation of cracks and damage, while offering a pathway for screening candidate PFMs and quantifying their lifespan under fusion-relevant conditions.

Microstructural Generative Inverse Models for Additive Manufacturing Digital Twins

arXiv:2610.30404v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Metal additive manufacturing (AM) offers substantial advantages to the fabrication of complex structural components, but its widespread adoption is slowed due to complexities in the process-structure-property relations which is the cause for uncertainty and drives risk. Accurately quantifying these complex relations solely via experimental workflows is time consuming and costly. Integrated computational materials and engineering (ICME) frameworks address this challenge by leveraging advanced computational tools, such as crystal plasticity finite element models (CPFEM), to predict critical mechanical properties directly from microstructural data. However, these frameworks require a large datasets that are often difficult to obtain, and their predictive accuracy depends on the availability of realistic AM microstructure representations. Generative deep learning models are a promising approach towards synthesizing AM microstructures which contain complex morphological and crystallographic features. Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs), in particular, show a strong potential to capture the inherent complexities of AM structures. In this work, we demonstrate that DDPMs can reliably synthesize digital-twin orientation-imaging micrographs, and we support this claim with quantitative evidence of the fidelity of the generated samples. Additionally, we establish a classifier-free guidance framework for solving complex inverse problems such as property-to-structure and process-to-structure mappings, and we showcase a potential application on a qualification-relevant uncertainty quantification problem. Our work highlights the potential of AI-based generative modeling as a key component of computational frameworks for accelerating AM materials qualification and certification.

Phase-Matched Chirps for Energy-Efficient Excitation of Magneto-Mechanical Resonators

arXiv:2610.31296v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Magneto-mechanical resonators (MMRs) are a recently proposed class of passive, wireless devices used for sensing and tracking. The operating principle of MMRs relies on an excitation of the sensor by oscillating magnetic fields, followed by an inductive readout to measure the sensor's dynamics. One challenge lies in generating efficient excitation signals to ensure maximum energy harvesting by the sensor. In this simulation study, we focus on chirped magnetic-field excitations and compare their performance with conventional constant-frequency excitation for both sinusoidal and rectangular waveforms. The results indicate varying levels of efficiency, with a sinusoidal linear down-chirp excitation signal yielding the largest deflection angle, offering the best potential for reducing the energy consumption and improving the temporal resolution of the measurement.
