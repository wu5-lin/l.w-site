Context as Infrastructure: Knowledge Compilation for Retrieval in Agentic Systems with Harnesses

arXiv:2610.41172v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Retrieval-augmented generation is becoming agentic. A search harness writes queries, reads results, reformulates, and decides when to stop. This largely solves accuracy: a harness with only BM25/grep and MiniMax M3 answers 85% of BrowseComp-Plus and 91% on multi-hop FRAMES in our runs, without compiled or vector indexes. It exposes a context problem: an undisciplined loop repeats queries, rereads documents, and fills the window with unused passages. The missing layer is knowledge compilation: a configurable ingestion pipeline where LLMs enrich documents with context no chunk contains alone--navigation hierarchies with claim-evidence leaves, entity graphs, timelines, mind maps, ontologies, wikis, and memory. Compilation is not a better retriever: under a competent harness, structural artifacts gave no detectable accuracy gain on FRAMES, and a PageIndex-style outline degraded at scale. It is a supply layer. A navigation tree whose leaves are claim-evidence pairs--the mechanism we measure--cuts query tokens by 91% when evidence suffices and adds 1.9% when it does not, a 38% average reduction; this implies a 2% break-even sufficiency rate and a measured rate near 43%. A decision gate cuts latency from 140 s to 30 s, though substitution's token saving is bought with retrieval rounds and carries its own latency cost. Other artifacts--ontology, graph, wiki, timeline, mind map, memory--pay off in deployment (governance, integration, onboarding, temporal orientation, continuity) where accuracy benchmarks cannot see them. We describe RAGFlow's pipeline and harness (open source at https://github.com/infiniflow/ragflow), which turns the loop into five disciplined stations and keeps the context entering the model the smallest sufficient set; together they form an agent-native database: a store that hands the agent compiled, governed context instead of a ranked list of passages.

Beyond CLIP: Calibrated (Few-Shot) Defect Detection With System One Decision Models

arXiv:2610.41199v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Cameras and vision sensors are widely used for product and process inspection in manufacturing environments. Traditionally, this process required thousands of images of both nominal and defective products to train such models. Recent research shows that vision transformer (ViT) models, such as CLIP, can reduce this requirement to 50-100 images per nominal and defective class. However, collecting even 50 defective images can be difficult in high-yield or low-volume manufacturing processes. In this paper, we examine the utility of two recently proposed structured decision models (Jev and Clef-Flash) in applications using as few as k=2 reference images per class at inference. In the process, we provide three main contributions: (a) a framework for k-shot learning of these models for image classification/inspection applications; (b) an agentic approach for automatically refining the text descriptions that guide image classification in these decision models; and (c) an approach to calibrate the resulting defect probabilities to improve their reliability for inspection decisions. We test these contributions in five manufacturing case studies. Using two reference images per class, both Jev and Clef-Flash outperform CLIP's test accuracies with the same references (0.75-1.00 [Jev] and 0.71-0.99 [Clef-Flash] vs 0.67-0.84 [CLIP]). Furthermore, we show that our agentic refinement of text descriptions significantly improves accuracy across 6 of 8 model-application combinations, and our calibration approach reduces the Brier score in 13 of the 15 model-application configurations. To support the reproducibility of our work, we provide our replication code and have created the R "decisions" package for evaluating additional models. We also provide a publicly available web app that allows practitioners to inspect their own images with no code.

From Evaluation to Evolution: Benchmarking Image Editing and Evolving Image Models

arXiv:2610.41210v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Real-world image editing requires coordinating multiple visual transformations with content preservation and reference constraints, yet aggregate scores can readily obscure unmet local requirements. Effective evaluation must therefore identify local failures and translate them into priorities for subsequent model improvement. We introduce WeGenEditBench, comprising 2,740 evaluation instances and 148 task types across single-image, text-intensive, and multi-reference editing. Pre-generated checklists shared across models decompose each request into atomic requirements; item-level deductions and visual evidence are established first and then summarized into scores for edit execution, preservation, visual quality, and task-specific correctness. To support the experience-acquisition component of recursive self-improvement (RSI), we develop WeGenEvoStudio, a unified multi-agent harness for RSI-oriented learning that integrates model-conditioned failure diagnosis, retrieval-grounded data construction, supervised adaptation, and re-evaluation. Among 15 evaluated configurations, GPT-Image-2 leads single-image editing with an Overall score of 4.076, while Seedream-5.0-Pro leads text-intensive editing at 4.291. Evolution experiments across four generation and editing benchmarks demonstrate substantial targeted gains: on their report-selected Top-5 single-image targets, FLUX.2 [dev] improves Overall from 1.083 to 3.517, while Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 gains 13.2%. Together, our benchmark and harness provide a foundation for developing more reliable image models through interpretable evaluation and feedback-driven learning.

Coarse3D: Controllable and Faithful Sparse Structure Generation for Image-to-3D

arXiv:2610.41231v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Coarse-to-fine 3D-native generation relies heavily on the first-stage sparse structure, which determines the global orientation and overall geometry for subsequent refinement. However, existing coarse generators suffer from two key limitations: their orientation is difficult to control due to ambiguous semantic canonicalization, and their structures are often inconsistent with input images due to weak 2D-3D grounding. We propose Coarse3D, a sparse-structure generation model that produces orientation-controllable and structurally faithful voxel geometry from arbitrary unposed images. Coarse3D introduces three designs: a first-frame anchored canonical space that aligns generation with the first input view while preserving object-centric normalization; a dual-tower DiT that couples generation with auxiliary feed-forward reconstruction to learn spatially grounded features; and spatial-prior guided attention that regularizes cross-attention with pixel-to-voxel correspondence. Coarse3D can directly replace the coarse stage of existing pipelines. Experiments show that it substantially improves sparse structure quality and consistently enhances final 3D generation, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

ANGLER: Verifier-Grounded Benchmarking with an Adaptive Attacker for Web Agents

arXiv:2610.41272v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Web agents are increasingly deployed to complete tasks autonomously on real websites, where adversarially injected content can induce them to perform unauthorized actions. Existing evaluations primarily use fixed attacks or focus on a single attack type, and evaluate attacks at the text level rather than through real state changes, thus underestimating how vulnerable agents are to diverse attacks. To address the gap, we introduce REEF, a benchmark of red-teaming executable environments with faithful verification for measuring the security vulnerabilities of web agents. REEF covers 9 verifier-grounded websites spanning travel, commerce, productivity, media, and communication, and pairs every authentic benign task with a malicious objective delivered through 5 attack types at both the user and the environment level. REEF's state-based verifiers provide the execution feedback that drives ANGLER, an adaptive near-miss-guided lure evolution framework for red-teaming. Rather than evaluating a fixed attack set, ANGLER uses outcomes from prior rollouts to guide LLM-based attack mutation and candidate selection, prioritizing near-miss seeds during refinement. Across 8 VLM-based web agents, ANGLER achieves an end-to-end attack success rate (ASR) of 20.36%, compared with 15.24% for the non-adaptive setting. Compared with attack-free execution on the same benign tasks, attacks generated by ANGLER reduce benign task success rate (BSR) from 61.9% to 44.4%.

In-Model Context Compression with Agentic Soft Tokens for Long-Horizon LLM Agents

arXiv:2610.41291v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Long-horizon LLM agents play an important role in solving complex and sustained tasks, with ever-growing interaction contexts. However, these accumulated contexts will raise substantial computational cost and obscure key decision-relevant evidence. Existing methods typically train external compressors or context-management agents to rewrite context as shorter hard-token sequences, but such external selection may not align with the LLM's evolving representation and is necessarily lossy. In this paper, we introduce Agentic Soft-Token Compression (ASTC), a training-free compression method using soft tokens within LLM agent for long-horizon tasks. Specifically, ASTC uses the model's native recurrent dynamics to determine the already absorbed information, leveraging the commonly-used hybrid Gated Delta Network (GDN)-attention. It then carries this information forward as agentic soft tokens while retaining exact values as hard tokens. The resulting compact sequence reduces later-layer computation and yields reusable KV caches, extending the savings across the agent's trajectory. Extensive experiments on long-horizon tasks show that ASTC matches or exceeds the uncompressed agent's accuracy with up to 50.68% fewer end-to-end Attention Pairs, while trained compressors require 15.4 to 103.7 times more Attention Pairs per successful task. These findings highlight agentic soft tokens as a model-native way to carry absorbed context forward as the task representation evolves.

ART of PIV: Agentic Real-Time Particle Image Velocimetry for Active Flow Control

arXiv:2610.41304v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Particle image velocimetry (PIV) measures fluid motion from images of tracer particles, but its accuracy depends on choices throughout the estimation pipeline, including image preprocessing, flow estimation, and postprocessing. These choices are typically left to expert tuning, and a fixed configuration can become less accurate as conditions change. This matters especially when PIV supplies feedback for real-time control, where measurements must be both accurate and fast, and flow conditions vary. We introduce ART of PIV -- short for Agentic Real-Time and also a nod to the art of tuning a PIV pipeline -- which automates pipeline choice for each image pair. A learned agent selects a configuration according to a chosen measurement objective from a library assembled for a specified speed budget. Across cross-correlation, classical optical-flow, and learning-based estimators, ART of PIV reduces average end-point error (AEPE) by 12.2-22.4 % relative to the best single estimator at matched speed, with gains extending to 1000-4000 image pair/s. In a simulated local velocity regulation task, it reduces mean normalized closed-loop cost by 13.24-13.59 percentage points relative to a tuned fixed estimator, with the same classical controller in both cases. In a tabletop water channel, we illustrate two ways to tune ART of PIV for a fluidic pinball of three rotating cylinders: selecting between complementary optical-flow tunings for accuracy, and selecting regions of interest with coherent rotation or shear using a common flow estimator. For the first variant, we train a DreamerV3 reinforcement learning controller directly in the physical world and demonstrate tracking of a prescribed flow field in the wake of a fluidic pinball.

Erase the Injected Region: Multimodal Prompt-Injection Defense with On-Policy Self-Distillation

arXiv:2610.41307v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Multimodal indirect prompt injection embeds malicious instructions in external media that also contains information needed for the user's task. Existing defenses can block the attack yet impair the model's use of that information, leaving a gap between attack defense and task preservation. We propose ERASE, an on-policy self-distillation method that constructs its reference by erasing the injected region in place. Modality-appropriate blank content replaces the injection while the surrounding media is preserved. A student reading attacked media learns from a frozen base model reading the erased view through full-distribution token-level feedback on its own rollouts. Across image, audio, and video on two model sizes, ERASE records near-zero observed attack success on the evaluated benchmark while retaining task utility close to the undefended clean-input level. On 3B audio, ERASE reaches 60.1% attacked-input accuracy compared with ARGUS's 33.1%, within 0.5 points of the undefended clean-input level. Experiments show that ERASE reduces injection dependence from 6.26 to 0.0095 on 3B audio while staying close to the base model on the erased input. Out-of-domain accuracy also stays near the base level. Our code will be made publicly available upon acceptance.
