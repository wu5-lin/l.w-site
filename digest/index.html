Tradeoff-Aware Territory Design: A Multi-Objective Decision System for Enterprise Customer-to-Seller Allocation

arXiv:2610.31701v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Enterprise customer journeys are shaped not only by product experiences and marketing, but also by recurring resource-allocation decisions. In B2B go-to-market (GTM) organizations, territory carving determines which accounts receive seller coverage, which seller-account relationships are preserved, and how limited field capacity is distributed. Yet the process often remains manual, making it difficult to compare opportunities, value continuity, and evaluate tradeoffs among growth, balance, capacity, and feasibility. We present a production decision system for tradeoff-aware customer-to-seller allocation in enterprise GTM planning. It integrates three components: a multidimensional account-value layer that combines predictive signals to score opportunity; an observational continuity-valuation layer that estimates the cost of breaking seller-account relationships using a synthetic-control-style counterfactual framework; and a multi-objective optimization layer that allocates coverage to high-value accounts while considering continuity, opportunity balance, and operational constraints. Deployed as a scenario-based decision-support system, the framework enables planners to inspect and compare allocation tradeoffs. Historical backtesting shows that optimizer-generated scenarios substantially improve assignment compliance while maintaining comparable seller-level opportunity distribution. In production planning, adopted scenarios improved both assignment compliance and opportunity balance relative to the previous baseline. Directing scarce seller capacity toward higher-value accounts also offers incremental revenue potential at enterprise scale. The key contribution is an integrated, inspectable framework that turns territory carving into a governable decision process.

Phronix: The Capability-Centric Learning Paradigm for Agents

arXiv:2610.31789v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Scientific computing increasingly relies on large language model-based agents for performance engineering tasks such as instrumentation, analysis, and workflow porting. However, agent effectiveness depends heavily on the surrounding harness, which typically relies on manually written skills and curated memory. This limits autonomous learning, forcing agents to repeatedly rediscover solutions rather than retain and reuse knowledge from prior executions. We introduce Phronix, a capability-centric learning paradigm that enables agentic harnesses to acquire, validate, and refine reusable capabilities from their own executions. Phronix operates through a predictor-validator-corrector loop: MCP tools and associated skills propose solutions, tool executions validate them, and observed outcomes refine the stored capabilities. It distills every observed interaction into a capability store and researches a failing task rather than abandoning it. First, such learned capabilities solve a recurring task on the first attempt in 93-95% of cases across every model, significantly reducing solution non-determinism and improving reliability. Second, Phronix turns an agent's own execution into 7.6-30.8 durable capabilities per task without user instruction, and creation falls below two once the needed capability exists. Third, warm-start runs consume only 0.38-0.40 million tokens per task, which is three to four times less than a single baseline attempt, while a cold-start costs no more than the baseline. On the 1000 Genome Pegasus workflow, instrumenting a production application for I/O performance analysis, Phronix passes all 11 correctness checks against six for the baseline harness, and also lifts the task solving rates of two cheaper models from 29% and 27% of SWE-bench tasks solved to 91% and 87%, respectively, matching the performance of the most expensive evaluated model.

PaperFormalizer: Author-in-the-Loop Autoformalization of Research Papers in Lean

arXiv:2610.31810v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Even a carefully proofread paper can leave small gaps in its proofs or its stated assumptions. We introduce PaperFormalizer, a Lean-based agentic pipeline that helps authors find and close such gaps in their own papers and turn each paper into a Lean-verified version. Starting from the LaTeX source, PaperFormalizer transcribes the paper into a dependency graph of statements, audits each statement in natural language for faithfulness, ambiguity and correctness, and then formalizes and proves the statements in Lean against Mathlib, optional domain libraries and the author's personal toolkit. Only the author knows what a statement is meant to say and how a formalization gap in a result is best fixed. The pipeline therefore stops and asks the author whenever a statement admits more than one reading, presents every repair, in natural language and TeX symbols, as a proposal that the author can accept, decline or redirect, and leaves to the author every decision that could potentially change what the paper claims. The output is an annotated and revised version of the paper, a report of the changes, and Lean code in which each declaration is linked to the statement it formalizes. The author's toolkit grows as more papers are formalized, and authors can share their verified papers so that researchers who build on them can reuse their Lean code. By making verified papers practical to produce, PaperFormalizer aims to make research that rests on mathematical proofs more robust. PaperFormalizer runs inside a coding agent (Claude Code or Codex) and is available at https://github.com/Jiyuan-Tan/PaperFormalizer. Papers with Lean code shared by users are available at https://paperformalizer.org.

Phonon evidence for a partial, re-entrant spin reorientation near room temperature in LaFe$_{1-x}$Ni$_x$O$_3$

arXiv:2610.31725v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Spin reorientation near room temperature offers a route to controlling antiferromagnetic order, yet LaFeO$3$, which lacks a magnetic rare-earth ion, is generally expected to retain its spin orientation along the $a$ axis below $T{\mathrm N}\approx740$~K. Here, we investigate LaFe$_{1-x}$Ni$_x$O$_3$ ($x=0$ and 0.1) using temperature-dependent magnetization and Raman spectroscopy over 200--400~K. Both compositions exhibit magnetic anomalies at 285.6 and 323~K, respectively, accompanied by correlated deviations of 12 Raman modes from their conventional anharmonic behavior. Principal component analysis and a global fitting approach reveal a common temperature-dependent phonon response, with mode-dependent amplitudes and signs. To interpret these anomalies, we develop a Landau-based spin-phonon coupling model describing complete, partial re-entrant, and incipient spin reorientation. Comparison with the experimental Raman profiles favors a partial, re-entrant rotation of the N'eel vector in both compositions, centered near 290~K. Ni substitution narrows the characteristic reorientation interval from approximately 116 to 84~K and enhances the magnetization anomaly by a factor of four. An additional feature near 262~K in LaFeO$_3$ is consistent with the Morin transition of trace $\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$. These findings suggest that partial spin reorientation can emerge without magnetic rare-earth ions and establish collective Raman phonon anomalies as a sensitive indirect probe of magnetic anisotropy changes in orthoferrites.

Nonadiabatic vibrational dynamics of CO on Pt(111) studied with first-principles many-body perturbation theory

arXiv:2610.31727v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We investigate the nonequilibrium vibrational dynamics of CO adsorbed on Pt(111), a prototypical adsorbate-surface system for ultrafast vibrational spectroscopy, using a first-principles framework that combines many-body perturbation theory with density functional theory. Within this methodology we explicitly account for electron-hole pair excitations and electron-mediated phonon-phonon coupling. Altermagnetism in Folded Iron Chain Systems

arXiv:2610.31732v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Altermagnetism, a moment-compensated magnetism with momentum-dependent spin splitting, has so far been realized primarily in N\'{e}el-type antiferromagnets. Here, we demonstrate altermagnetism in folded iron-chain systems with block-type magnetic order (e.g., $\uparrow \uparrow \downarrow \downarrow$). In the strictly one-dimensional (1D) case, altermagnetism is forbidden by the $[C_2||{\cal T}]$ symmetry, but it can emerge when such chains are stacked into a quasi-1D array. To generate altermagnetic spin splitting via this chain-stacking mechanism, two essential ingredients are required: a block-type spin order that spontaneously breaks the combined parity-time reversal symmetry, and finite inter-chain hopping. Using first-principles calculations, we identify the K$_3$Fe$_2$Se$_4$ family as a promising platform that realizes this mechanism, hosting a $d$-wave altermagnet even without lattice $C_{4}$ symmetry. Moreover, owing to its lower symmetry, the block-type folded chain exhibits an isotropic altermagnetic spin splitting, which may manifest as an altermagnetic wrapping effect. Our findings unify real-space block magnetism and reciprocal-space altermagnetism, establishing folded iron chains as a tunable platform for zero-moment spintronic applications.

Thermoelectric properties and modulation doping of ultrawide-bandgap nitride monolayers

arXiv:2610.31777v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Two-dimensional III-nitride alloys provide a composition-tunable platform for exploring thermoelectric transport and intrinsic electric-field tolerance. We combine first-principles electronic-structure calculations with semiclassical transport for ordered monolayer B$_{x}$Al$_{1-x}$N and B$_{x}$Ga$_{1-x}$N. State-dependent momentum relaxation times computed with AMSET include acoustic deformation-potential and piezoelectric scattering. Using a common, temperature-independent lattice thermal conductivity of 550 W/mK, we predict maximum thermoelectric figure of merit ZT>2.5 for selected p- and n-type monolayers and ZT>2 at 300~K for n-type B$_{0.25}$Ga$_{0.75}$N. Composition-dependent band curvature, screening, and scattering help interpret the different p- and n-type responses. A separate phenomenological model using the band gap and maximum phonon frequency predicts intrinsic breakdown fields of 20.06 and 20.74 MVcm for the Al- and Ga-containing x=0.75 alloys, respectively. These results motivate further investigation for thermoelectric and high-field power-electronic applications. The ZT values apply to the specified scattering and lattice-conductivity models, while the breakdown estimates require validation for monolayers and do not resolve field direction.

Perfectly aperiodic asymmetric scalable metasurface based on the hat monotile

arXiv:2610.32467v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Metasurfaces are conventionally designed on periodic lattices, which renders their electromagnetic response spatially uniform and leaves the spatial arrangement itself unused as an information-bearing design resource. In this study, we propose and numerically validate a metasurface built on an aperiodic monotile: the recently discovered "hat" polykite, a single tile mathematically guaranteed to tile the plane only nonperiodically. The twelve distinguishable orientations of the hat serve as a natural design basis; each carries resonant elements tuned to one of twelve absorption frequencies between 4.600 and 4.914 GHz, transcribing the provable aperiodicity and mirror asymmetry of the geometry into the electromagnetic response. Gaussian-beam scanning over a compositionally unique 6 times 6 grid yields irregular, position-dependent electromagnetic signatures whose pairwise separations reach beyond 6 dB. A shift-correlation analysis in which the cosine similarity never exceeds 0.5 confirms that the response inherits the absence of translational order from the tiling. Compatible with standard printed-circuit fabrication, the aperiodic metasurface design establishes a deterministic route toward position-identifiable electromagnetic environments for localization, chipless identification, and integrated sensing and communication.

A Self-Consistent Two-Carrier Drift-Diffusion Framework for p-n Junctions: Extended Diode Equation, Power Dissipation, and Base Transit Time

arXiv:2610.32112v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The classical theory of p-n junctions derives the current-voltage characteristic from minority-carrier diffusion alone: majority carriers are treated as an inert background, the quasi-neutral base as field-free, and the junction voltage as equal to the applied bias. These premises fail together under high bias and high-level injection, where the observed current roll-off is reproduced only by appending an empirical series resistance outside the junction physics. Here we develop a self-consistent two-carrier drift-diffusion framework that introduces no assumption beyond the classical ones. Excess majority carriers are shown to be electrostatically forced transport participants: the ambipolar diffusion equation, closed by exact two-sided boundary conditions, yields a unified excess-carrier profile. The resulting base field separates into an ohmic and a Dember component, and all four current components follow in closed form, their sum being position independent. At device level the framework closes into an extended diode equation that contains the Shockley equation as its low-injection, long-base limit and reproduces the n=2 roll-off intrinsically. The junction voltage emerges as a self-limiting drop confined to the space-charge region and separated from the applied bias by exact base drops. Two device-level corrections are quantified: at high current, 75-90% of the dissipation migrates from the junction into the quasi-neutral base, and field-assisted acceleration shortens the base transit time by up to a factor of six. Three parameter-free predictions follow: a universal ideality-factor crossover that passes through 3/2 when the boundary excess equals the doping, a high-injection onset voltage that scales as 119 mV per decade of doping, and a geometry factor that survives into high injection.

Photon-Number Discrimination from Increasing Time-of-Arrival Separation in Wide Superconducting Microstrip Single-Photon Detectors

arXiv:2610.32154v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Measurements of photon-number-dependent time-of-arrival (TOA) distributions in wide NbN superconducting microstrip single-photon detectors (SMSPDs) are presented. Two \SI{10}{\um}-long devices with widths of \SI{3.2}{\um} and \SI{6.4}{\um} are characterized under pulsed \SI{515}{nm} excitation. The TOA histograms are described by sums of exponentially modified Gaussian (EMG) components, with the wider device exhibiting components assigned to photon numbers up to \(n=8\). Adjacent TOA peaks become more distinguishable relative to their widths at higher photon number, in contrast to the compression of neighboring photon-number timing shifts observed in conventional narrow-wire SNSPDs. For the components assigned to \(n=7\) and \(n=8\), the FWHM-normalized peak separation is \(2.15\), and the fitted EMG distributions yield an equal-prior pairwise TOA-assignment fidelity of \(99.6\%\). The EMG exponential-tail parameter \(\tau_n\) decreases strongly with photon number, while the Gaussian-location parameter \(\mu_n\) continues to shift between neighboring components. A phenomenological model motivated by absorption-site-dependent vortex processes and collective current redistribution captures these timing trends. These results support wide SMSPDs as a platform for TOA-based photon-number discrimination at high photon number, relevant to multiphoton-state characterization and photonic quantum information processing. The same geometry also provides a setting to examine how the spatial distribution of photon absorption affects vortex-assisted detection.
