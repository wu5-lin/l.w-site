Automatic Construction of Strong Simple Baselines

arXiv:2610.40700v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: To rigorously establish the value of a novel deep learning method, it must be compared against appropriate baselines. As deep learning has become increasingly common in the physical and life sciences, there have been numerous cases in which apparent progress has turned out to be an artifact of omitted or missing baselines. In this work, we investigate whether this problem can be mitigated by using large language models to automate the construction of baselines. We assemble 36 supervised classification and regression tasks from peer-reviewed studies in Nature, Science, and Cell-family journals that investigate novel deep learning methods for scientific applications. We then use a language model to construct simple baselines for each task. These automatically constructed baselines are strong, recovering a median of 95.5% (mean 85.7%) of the gap between a trivial predictor and the score of the deep learning model. The median simple baseline matches or exceeds the performance of the deep learning model in 12 studies. We further examine the variation across scientific domains and publication venues and we characterize the effects of test-time compute and access to different levels of study context. As human peer review has struggled to keep up with the rapidly increasing volume of research articles in recent years, our results suggest that automated baseline construction is a practical and scalable check on deep learning contributions in the physical and life sciences.

Bayesian Distribution System State Estimation with Flow Matching

arXiv:2610.40728v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Medium-voltage feeders have few real-time measurements, so distribution system state estimation (DSSE) rests on pseudo-measurements of the secondary substations, a prior in Bayesian terms. Weighted least squares keeps the power flow exact but reduces this prior to a mean and variance, while supervised deep learning estimators also learn the power flow and are tied to one measurement set. We propose PoSE (posterior sampling for state estimation), which keeps the Bayesian structure of DSSE and learns only what the distribution system operator does not know, namely the distribution of substation injections, modelled by a neural network trained with flow matching on historical data. The power flow equations, grid topology and measurements enter only at sampling, through a noise-aware Kalman-type correction. The neural network therefore never approximates the power flow, but every posterior sample is a physically consistent operating point, and one trained prior serves any measurement set, noise level and topology. On the IEEE 33-bus feeder, PoSE lowers the probabilistic injection error (CRPS) by 6.7-7.5% against a full-covariance Gaussian prior in all 12 tested settings. It provides reliable uncertainty: the 90% intervals contain the true injection almost exactly as often as exact posterior samples would, whereas those of supervised estimators contain it in only 37-78% of cases. On the 240-bus unbalanced three-phase Iowa feeder, it gives the lowest injection, voltage and branch-current errors of all estimators. A fast variant matches this accuracy at 0.2-0.6s per snapshot and, without retraining, beats Gaussian priors and retrained supervised estimators on seven reconfigured topologies.

Don't Just Impute, Detect: Task-Aware Diffusion Pipelines for Edge Anomaly Detection

arXiv:2610.40732v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) edge pipelines routinely lose contiguous blocks of multi-sensor data to network dropouts and hardware faults. The standard remedy is a disjointed "impute-then-detect" pipeline whose imputer is trained only for Mean Squared Error (MSE). Such an imputer fills a missing block with the most probable normal pattern: a partially observed anomaly is smoothed back toward normality, and boundary artifacts on normal data raise false alarms. We call this imputation bias and propose the Task-Aware Diffusion Refiner (TADR), a conditional diffusion refiner trained against the loss landscape of a frozen downstream detector. Because back-propagating through the sampling trajectory is intractable, the task-aware terms act on the one-step clean-window estimate that the noise prediction already yields, so training costs no more than plain diffusion training. Under strict Point-to-Point evaluation over five seeds with paired significance tests, TADR raises F1 by $0.338$ over the unrefined detector on SMD ($p = 0.0015$), and the anomaly enhancement objective alone contributes $0.463$ in ablation. We also characterise when the method helps: the gain tracks the continuous, mutually redundant channels available and the anomaly evidence the outage removes, and it is absent on telemetry datasets dominated by one-hot command channels and on PSM. At ten sampling steps TADR runs in $41.4$ ms per window on a single CPU core, sustaining about $24$ concurrent 1 Hz streams from a $4.03$ MB model. Code and per-seed results are available at https://github.com/int-park422/TADR.

Unsupervised Feature Selection Using Local Intrinsic Dimensionality -- Extended Version

arXiv:2610.40736v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: High dimensionality degrades both the efficiency and the effectiveness of learning algorithms, and in many applications no class labels are available to guide dimensionality reduction. This paper proposes LIDFS, an unsupervised feature selection method based on Local Intrinsic Dimensionality (LID). The method builds on the formal equivalence between LID and the indiscriminability of distance measures. The data matrix is transposed so that each feature is treated as a point, and the LID of each feature is estimated by maximum likelihood from the distances to its $k$ nearest neighboring features. Features with lower LID exhibit greater local discriminability and receive higher importance scores. A refinement step then retains one representative per feature neighborhood and demotes locally redundant features. The result is a complete ranking from which any number of features can be selected without rerunning the procedure. LIDFS does not use any information about labels or the number of classes, and runs in $\mathcal{O}(d^2 n)$ time for $n$ instances and $d$ features. LIDFS is compared with classic and state-of-the-art methods and a random baseline on 23 high-dimensional datasets, the classic benchmark collection. The evaluation uses classification accuracy and AUC with five downstream classifiers, as well as clustering accuracy and normalized mutual information, over both full and aggressive selection ranges. LIDFS ranks first in the supervised evaluations and first or tied second in the clustering-based evaluations. It belongs to the top statistical group together with methods that require the number of classes, and it runs faster than its closest competitor. Several recent methods, by contrast, fail to outperform the random baseline. A sensitivity analysis shows that small neighborhood sizes yield the best results.

SKFitter: Symmetry resolved Slater Koster tight-binding models from first principles for s, p, d and f electrons, spin orbit coupling and magnetism

arXiv:2610.40729v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Constructing accurate and physically interpretable tight binding (TB) Hamiltonians from first principles calculations remains challenging, particularly for materials exhibiting complex orbital hybridization, spin orbit coupling (SOC), and magnetic order. SKFitter is an open-source Python framework for constructing and optimizing symmetry resolved Slater Koster TB models directly from density functional theory (DFT) calculations performed using Quantum ESPRESSO or VASP. The framework supports s to f orbitals, incorporates on site SOC and site resolved magnetic exchange interactions, and accommodates both orthogonal and nonorthogonal basis. A central feature is the symmetry resolved parameterization of orbital manifolds, which allows independent two centre hopping integrals between symmetry allowed orbital groups and across distinct neighbour shells. SKFitter also identifies missing orbital contributions and guide systematic basis set refinement and optimizes parameters through weights. The resulting Hamiltonian can be used to evaluate anomalous (AHC) and spin Hall conductivities (SHC), and topological invariants. We demonstrate the framework using eight representative materials spanning semimetals, semiconductors, transition metals, f-electron compounds, and magnetic systems. Symmetry resolved models achieve band energy errors of 9 to 41 meV within 1 eV of the Fermi level. The fitted Hamiltonians reproduce characteristic features of the SHC response of Pt, the SOC dependence of the AHC of Fe, and the momentum dependent altermagnetic spin splitting of MnTe without any altermagnet specific interaction. For EuMn2Bi2, projection guided orbital refinement reduces the near Fermi level fitting error from 120 to 32 meV. Together, these results establish SKFitter as a flexible and reproducible framework for developing symmetry consistent, physically interpretable TB models of complex materials.

Thermal Stability and Structural Relaxation Kinetics of Commercially Pure Titanium

arXiv:2610.40737v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: This work explores the structural relaxation of cryorolled commercially pure titanium subjected to subsequent annealing.Nanostructured specimens with an average grain size of ~50 nm were produced by plastic deformation via cryogenic rolling at 100 K to a true strain of 1.20, followed by annealing at 425 K, 525 K, 725 K, and 945 K. Microstructural evolution was examined using electron microscopy and X-ray diffraction. Cryogenic rolling was found to induce pronounced grain fragmentation, with coherent scattering regions (CSRs) of 30-50 nm dominating the substructure. X-ray diffraction analysis revealed that annealing drives crystallite growth: a slight increase at 525 K, accelerated coarsening at 725 K, and complete recrystallization at 945 K. The kinetics of grain growth follow the classical Lifshitz-Slyozov-Wagner model, with a growth exponent of ~4, reflecting the role of triple junctions and defect-controlled boundary diffusion. The activation energy, estimated at ~97 kJ/mol, corresponds to typical values for grain boundary diffusion in {\alpha}-Ti, confirming that cryogenic deformation produces a highly non-equilibrium microstructure whose relaxation is strongly temperature dependent.

For instance, these crystallites in the glass of turquoise Bohemian seed beads are known to play a significant role in the process of internal-stress driven corrosion of the glass causing deterioration and destruction of bead embroidered museum exhibits, especially articles made in the early 19th century. In art materials science, KSS has been known since the mid-2010s only for its destructive effects on historical glass objects, but as an optical material it has long been studied. KSS belongs to the group of compounds isomorphic to KTiOPO$_4$ (KTP) and, like other compounds from this group, has anomalies in dielectric constant. The article presents the results of a study of orthorhombic KSS crystals using a number of spectroscopic techniques. The constituent peaks of the spectral bands are analyzed and their properties are summarized in tables; the allowed direct and indirect band gaps of KSS are determined. The presented data are necessary both for the correct spectral analysis of historical and artistic glass and enamels, during the production of which this substance was sometimes synthesized in glass, and for a more in-depth study of crystals of the KTP group.

Kibble-Zurek dynamics beyond the thermodynamic limit

arXiv:2610.40755v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The Kibble-Zurek mechanism (KZM) predicts the universal scaling of topological defect densities generated during continuous phase transitions, a result derived in the thermodynamic limit of homogeneous systems. Yet real materials are intrinsically finite and heterogeneous, raising the question of how nonequilibrium universality survives outside this idealized limit. Here we establish an ensemble-finite-size extension of the KZM that describes critical dynamics in collections of independent finite subsystems with distributed sizes. We test this framework using a polycrystalline model system undergoing a symmetry-breaking transition. We find that the density of topological vortices follows an apparent power law with an exponent approximately half that observed in single crystals. This reduced exponent emerges quantitatively from averaging over the experimentally measured grain-size distribution, without fitting to the measured vortex densities. Our results demonstrate that finite-size effects do not simply suppress KZ scaling but reorganize it into an emergent statistical scaling regime governed by system-size heterogeneity. This establishes microstructure and geometry as control parameters of nonequilibrium critical dynamics and provides a general strategy to engineer scaling behaviour in complex materials.

Complete Au-free Metallization Platform for UWBG Al$_{0.65}$Ga$_{0.35}$N channel MOS-HEMTs using Ti/Al/Ti Ohmic Contacts and Refractory TiN/Pt Gates

arXiv:2610.40993v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Au-free ohmic contacts and refractory gate stacks are critical for integrating ultra-wide-bandgap (UWBG) Al-rich AlGaN transistors with Si-CMOS technology. In this work, we demonstrate, to our knowledge, the first complete Au-free metallization platform for Al$_{0.65}$Ga$_{0.35}$N-channel MOS-HEMTs, combining electron-beam-evaporated and sputtered Ti/Al/Ti ohmic contacts with sputtered TiN and Pt gate metals. The Ti/Al/Ti contacts achieved a contact resistance of approximately 7.5--7.7 $\Omega\cdot$mm while being fully Au-free. This contact resistance is achieved using a 600 $^\circ$C anneal, substantially below the 850--950 $^\circ$C typically required for Au-capped Ti/Al/X/Au contacts on Al-rich AlGaN, indicating a reduced thermal budget and improved compatibility with silicon-CMOS foundries. TiN gates deliver drain-current density up to 450 mA/mm at $V_{\mathrm{th}} = -13$ V, whereas Pt gates shift $V_{\mathrm{th}}$ to -2.5 V with a reduced current drive of 220 mA/mm, reflecting the effects of gate work function. TiN-gated devices exhibit lower current degradation under pulsed operation and a higher critical breakdown field ($E_{\mathrm{c}} \approx 3.5$ MV/cm) than Pt-gated devices ($E_{\mathrm{c}} \approx 2.98$ MV/cm), measured without field plating at $L_{\mathrm{GD}} = 2.3$ $\mu$m. Collectively, these results establish a low-thermal-budget, fully Au-free, CMOS-compatible metallization platform for high-performance Al-rich AlGaN UWBG power electronics.

Wideband Near- and Far-Field Double-Bounce Physical Optics via Co-Area Range Profile Extraction

arXiv:2610.41812v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Wideband evaluation of the Radar Cross Section (RCS) and the underlying range profile of a target, including its secondary double-bounce fields, remains a computational bottleneck even for modern high-frequency solvers. This paper presents a framework for evaluating the wideband range profile under the Physical Optics (PO) approximation without repeated frequency-domain iterations. Using phase level-set integration via the Co-Area formula, we project the single- and double-bounce scattered fields into frequency-independent Geometric Range Profiles (GRPs), valid in both the far-field and near-field regions. The approach builds on the Radon Transform interpretation of Physical Optics scattering. Unlike point-sampling methods, our discrete implementation bins the profile in range while retaining a complex-valued representation that encodes sub-bin phase corrections, so the field can be evaluated accurately at frequencies well beyond the nominal mesh resolution. We also derive a corresponding time-domain formulation for the scattered transient response to analytic incident pulses. We further show that the classical second-derivative area law for far-field transient scattering extends into the near field as a two-term expression in the derivatives of an isochrone projected area, which reduces to the classical law as the additional induction term decays with range.

Magnetic Johnson noise from arbitrarily shaped conductors: experimental validation of the fluctuation-dissipation finite-element method

arXiv:2610.42113v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Magnetic Johnson noise--magnetic noise caused by thermal current fluctuations in conducting materials--can interfere with measurements and limit their sensitivity. As the sensitivity of experiments increases, it becomes increasingly important to accurately estimate the magnetic Johnson noise from experimental components with complex shapes during both the design and analysis phases. A particularly powerful approach is to combine the fluctuation-dissipation theorem with finite-element calculations. This versatile approach can be used to calculate the magnetic Johnson noise from conductors of arbitrary geometry for a wide range of frequencies and for magnetic field detectors with general geometries, such as a finite-sized loop or a gradiometer. Using a SQUID gradiometer array, we measured the magnetic Johnson noise from conductors with complex geometries and compared the results with calculations performed using this method. The calculations reproduce both the measured noise amplitude and its frequency dependence, providing an experimental validation of this method. We also performed a detailed analysis of how the geometry and material parameters affect the frequency dependence of magnetic Johnson noise.

Intensity-Domain Demultiplexing of Orbital Angular Momentum Channels for Optical Communication

arXiv:2610.41259v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Orbital angular momentum (OAM) multiplexing has been limited in practice by the complexity and optical loss of the elements that separate the channels at the receiver side. Here we adopt the azimuthal harmonics of OAM petal patterns as the demultiplexing basis, which makes the receiver the optical counterpart of the circular antenna arrays used in radio OAM links: the petal pattern of topological charge $\ell$ carries a single azimuthal harmonic of order $2\ell$, so one Fourier transform of the azimuthal intensity profile returns every channel, without gratings or mode sorters.
