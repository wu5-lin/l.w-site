Inverse Modeling for Laser Pulse Shape Design in Inertial Confinement Fusion

arXiv:2610.33096v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The achievement of practical fusion energy remains one of the most pressing unsolved scientific challenges, with immense implications for carbon-free power. A critical factor in the success of Inertial Confinement Fusion (ICF) is the design of a Laser Pulse Shape (LP) that can optimally drive implosions under stringent physical constraints. Traditional LP design relies on computationally expensive simulations and manual iterative refinement. We introduce the ICF Laser Pulse Shape Design System (LPDS), a generative inverse modeling framework that maps desired outcomes and target pellet configurations directly to optimized LPs. Crucially, we design a multi-objective loss function to ensure the generated LPs satisfy fundamental physical constraints and experimental requirements. Furthermore, we present constraint-conditioning, inpainting, and gradient-based LP editing mechanisms for fine-grained control over specific pulse characteristics during generation. Moreover, we study the transfer of our framework to a limited set of real experimental shots. Our method establishes a data-driven inverse design framework for LP design in ICF, contributing to the advancement of practical and sustainable fusion energy.

IRON-Meme: A Benchmark for Structured Understanding of Identities and Roles in Online Meme Narratives

arXiv:2610.33097v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: As polarization grows on social media, understanding how political memes shape online narratives has become increasingly important. Previous research has relied on either qualitative frameworks that are hard to scale or computational methods that lack theoretical grounding. Both approaches treat narrative categories in isolation and focus exclusively on moral portrayals of identity. To address these limitations, we introduce a framework grounded in social science theories, encompassing three categories: social identities (such as gender, politics, and ethnicity), viewpoints (ingroup and outgroup perspectives), and narrative roles (moral character, intelligence, status, and power). Building on this framework, we present IRON-Meme, a human-annotated benchmark comprising 1,110 political memes across substantially more templates than existing benchmarks. Our analysis of IRON-Meme shows that most memes assign derogatory roles and that identities, roles, and templates are systematically interconnected. Evaluation of nine vision-language models indicates that frontier-scale and reasoning models achieve the highest performance, while accuracy on social identities and narrative roles decreases for less frequent labels. Incorporating template background knowledge does not enhance narrative role classification, suggesting that meme interpretation relies on how memes are adapted for specific communicative purposes. We make IRON-Meme and our code publicly available.

Prospect Theory in Large Language Models: A Meta-Analysis of Loss Aversion, Diminishing Sensitivity, and Probability Weighting

arXiv:2610.33103v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Prospect theory splits risky choice into three parts: loss aversion, measured by the relative weight of losses to gains, $\lambda$ (e.g., $\lambda = 2$ implies that losses weigh twice as much as gains), diminishing sensitivity to the size of outcomes (i.e., the curvature and linearity of the value function), and the nonlinear weighting of probabilities. I examined whether Large Language Models (LLMs) display the same properties as people by meta-analyzing 102 fitted estimates from 11 studies covering 32 language models. The three components emerge differently. Loss aversion is modest: the pooled $\lambda$ is 1.26 (95% CI 1.06 to 1.46), far below the strongest human meta-analytic estimate ($\lambda = 1.96$) and compatible with more conservative ones (1.07 and 1.31). Matched by design, LLMs are compatible with people when gains and losses are symmetric ($\lambda = 1.15$ against 1.07), but well below them when losses are smaller than gains (1.46 against 2.3), where human loss aversion is strongest. Value functions are close to linear rather than concave under the Tversky-Kahneman method (1.01, against a human value of 0.67), but more concave than in people under the Tanaka method (0.52, against 0.67), so diminishing sensitivity depends on how it is measured. Probability weighting shows a human-like inverse S shape ($\gamma = 0.81$ under Tversky-Kahneman and 0.84 under the Tanaka method), though on few studies. In sum, LLMs show little loss aversion and little diminishing sensitivity, but retain human-like probability distortion, yet these generalizations shift when looking at specific model families and fitting methods.

Artificial Intelligence in Mechanical Engineering: Foundations, Opportunities, Challenges, and Responsibilities

arXiv:2610.33105v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping mechanical engineering (ME), from modeling, simulation, and optimization to diagnostics, design, and technical communication. Drawing on a round-table at the 28th ABCM International Congress on Mechanical Engineering (COBEM 2025), this article presents a structured perspective for the Brazilian and international ME communities. Rather than an exhaustive survey, it examines how AI can enhance engineering practice without compromising scientific rigor. We distinguish statistical learning from human cognition and discuss the capabilities, limitations, and emerging agentic forms of AI. We then consider industrial adoption, workforce changes, and AI-assisted scientific discovery, alongside the energy and infrastructure demands of AI computation and their implications for sustainability and thermal management. The discussion emphasizes the integration of physical knowledge, structured reasoning, and domain expertise into AI systems, particularly in safety-relevant applications. Topics include physics-informed and hybrid learning, physically constrained modeling, validation, uncertainty quantification, and certification. We also examine educational, epistemological, and ethical challenges, including curriculum reform, human-agent collaboration, professional accountability, and the preservation of engineering knowledge. We conclude with principles for responsible AI adoption in ME: physical consistency, rigorous validation, explicit treatment of uncertainty, energy sustainability, human oversight, and ethical responsibility. AI should complement, not replace, engineering judgment.

Collaborative In-Context Learning for Indoor Radio Map Estimation

arXiv:2610.31702v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Indoor radio map estimation (RME) is essential for wireless applications such as coverage planning, localization, and network deployment. However, most existing methods follow a supervised learning paradigm that requires costly labeled data and task-specific model training. Moreover, indoor environments involve privacy-sensitive information, such as building layouts and structures, that often cannot be centralized. To address these challenges, we make the first attempt to formulate indoor RME as a collaborative in-context learning (ICL) problem. We propose Poly-Ripple, a framework in which multiple clients perform ICL using large autoregressive vision models (LAVMs) without model updates. Each client is prompted by its private demonstrations and predicts the radio map for the same scene query. To capture the causal structure of wireless propagation outward from the transmitter (Tx), we adopt ripple-ordered autoregressive modeling. We further introduce importance-aware aggregation to exploit heterogeneous contextual knowledge distributed across clients under the context length constraint. We provide theoretical analysis and conduct extensive experiments on a public indoor RME benchmark. The experimental results show that Poly-Ripple consistently outperforms representative ICL baselines, while aligning with our theoretical analysis.

The Path to NDE 4.0: A Review of Algorithms, Techniques, and Strategies for Autonomous Inspection

arXiv:2610.31704v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Non-Destructive Inspection (NDI) is a cornerstone of modern quality assurance, critical for ensuring the safety and reliability of infrastructure and manufactured components without causing damage. This paper presents a comprehensive review of the current NDI landscape, analyzing the field through four distinct lenses, namely, computational algorithms, physical techniques, industrial applications, and research objectives. We synthesize a broad range of academic literature to illustrate the rapid evolution from manual, analog interpretation to a digitized, data-centric paradigm known as NDE 4.0. Our analysis highlights the dominance of ultrasound and visual inspection as primary physical techniques, while revealing a significant shift toward deep learning and generative models for automated defect recognition. Despite these technological strides, the industry faces critical bottlenecks, notably the scarcity of standardized open-access datasets and the "black box" nature of AI models, which hinders widespread adoption. By charting these developments and identifying key research gaps, such as the need for explainable AI (XAI) and standardized digital twin protocols, this review offers a strategic roadmap for future research. It concludes that the future of NDI research should focus on the successful integration of intelligent, autonomous fusion of inspection data with advanced sensor technologies into asset lifecycle management.

Does LeWorldModel Preserve Local Action Geometry?

arXiv:2610.31716v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We examine how closely pretrained LeWorldModel (LeWM) reproduces the effects of small action changes. Using matched simulator and model responses, we measure action sensitivity with Gram matrices at three perturbation scales. Across nearly a thousand states from Reacher and OGBench Cube, the most sensitive action directions differ by median angles of about $36^\circ$ and $42^\circ$, respectively, at the smallest scale. Increasing the perturbation tenfold reduces median normalized Gram discrepancy by roughly 70\% on Reacher and 47\% on Cube. Geometry measured in pixels and embeddings varies more across scales than the chosen physical-state geometry. On held-out directions, environment Grams achieve median embedding response errors of 17\% and 28\%, compared with 26\% and 38\% for an isotropic reference with the same trace. LeWM's errors are higher at this scale. These findings support scale-specific evaluation of action sensitivity; its effect on control remains an open question.

Towards Adversarially Robust Multigraph Neural Networks for Financial Crime Analysis

arXiv:2610.31718v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Financial transaction networks are naturally represented as multigraphs, where repeated transactions between the same pair of accounts are modeled as distinct edges. Recently proposed multigraph neural network architectures leverage this structure explicitly to achieve a strong performance on anti-money laundering (AML) tasks. However, their robustness to adversaries that manipulate transaction structures to evade detection remains largely unexplored. We present the first scalable framework for structural attacks and adversarial training of multigraph neural networks using node-edge incidence matrices, adapting projected gradient descent and projected randomized block coordinate descent methods to optimize structural perturbations over such matrices. We further introduce a cycle-based regularizer that penalizes changes in the global counts of directed 2- and 3-cycles, providing a structural measure of attack plausibility. Experiments on synthetic pattern-detection and realistic AML datasets show that our learned attacks substantially outperform non-learnable perturbation baselines in reducing detection performance (PR-AUC), while adversarial training consistently improves robustness against these attacks. These results demonstrate that multigraph neural networks are vulnerable to learned structural attacks and that adversarial training provides an effective defense. Our code is available at https://github.com/denheijmans/robust_multignn.

On the Doppler Effect in Static Materials: Vortices and Black, White and Worm-Hole Like Wave Trajectories

arXiv:2610.33141v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We demonstrate the Doppler effect in a static material. The effect is achieved with a network of thin waveguides whose lengths depend on the direction of travel, chosen to simulate the compression and expansion of the wave experienced by a source travelling with velocity $v$. Each edge of length $\ell$ becomes a pair of waveguides of lengths $\ell/(1\mp v/c)$, one for each direction of travel. On a lattice this gives the field pattern of a moving source. To demonstrate the effect, we design a vortex, an analogue black hole and wormhole.

Evolution of microstructure and crystallographic texture in a novel Zr-4Nb-2Al alloy during hot rotary swaging

arXiv:2610.33247v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A multi-step induction-assisted hot rotary swaging route was developed for the novel dual-phase Zr-4Nb-2Al alloy to investigate the evolution of microstructure, crystallographic texture, and mechanical properties during sequential deformation. Phase-transformation temperatures determined by electrical resistivity measurements were used to select processing conditions in the single-phase $\beta$ field at 880{\deg}C and the $\alpha+\beta$ field at 780{\deg}C. Induction heating with thermographic temperature control enabled reduction of the initial 12 mm rod to 6.3 mm, corresponding to a total area reduction of 72%. Swaging in the $\beta$ field refined millimetre-sized prior-$\beta$ grains to approximately 100 $\mu$m, while subsequent cooling produced a fine lamellar $\alpha+\beta$ microstructure. Further deformation in the $\alpha+\beta$ field disrupted the lamellar structure, promoted globularisation and recrystallisation of the $\alpha$ phase, and refined the $\beta$ phase. The microstructural evolution was accompanied by a pronounced $\langle 11\bar{2}0\rangle_{\alpha}\parallel\mathrm{SD}$ fibre texture in the $\alpha$ phase, whereas the $\beta$ phase exhibited predominantly $\langle 110\rangle_{\beta}\parallel\mathrm{SD}$ together with a $\langle 111\rangle_{\beta}\parallel\mathrm{SD}$ component. The phase constitution remained comparatively stable, with approximately 12-15 wt.% retained $\beta$ throughout processing. Subsequent $\alpha+\beta$ swaging decreased the compressive yield strength from 1103 to 839 MPa while increasing plastic deformability, with a maximum fracture strain of 32%. Radial hardness heterogeneity was progressively eliminated. These results demonstrate that induction-assisted rotary swaging in selected phase fields provides an effective route for tailoring microstructure, texture, and mechanical response in dual-phase zirconium alloys.

Winding-Controlled Topological Descendants of Antialtermagnets

arXiv:2610.33252v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Odd-parity spin splitting constrains magnetic band crossings but does not determine their topological descendants. We show that the Chern contribution of an isolated inverted sector is controlled by the phase winding of its transverse mass pair: when the masses and tangential velocities reverse together, contributions from successive Dirac points add rather than cancel. This distinguishes topological winding from the polynomial degree labeling the magnetic parent. For specified complementary masses, periodic tetragonal $h$- and hexagonal $j$-wave models host eight and twelve same-chirality Weyl points on a momentum plane, with gapped-slice Chern numbers $|C|=4$ and $6$, respectively. A microscopic four-band $p$-wave model identifies an antiunitary obstruction to first-order transverse masses and demonstrates a symmetry-broken $C=1$ phase at quarter filling. First-principles calculations for the proposed coplanar spin-vortex texture of LaMnAu$_5$ reveal the $h$-wave nodal pattern, its anisotropic spin--orbit reconstruction, and a mirror-channel spectral response that reverses under opposite spin rotations. Together, these results distinguish the symmetry of an odd-parity magnetic parent from the mass structure controlling the topology of its descendants.
