Counting the Bandages: On measuring the time that AI saves

arXiv:2610.35942v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: I consider what it means for AI assistance to save time. I contrast a stopwatch view, on which time saved is whatever the clock records, with a ledger view, on which it is a counterfactual that has to be read next to the errors the work lets through, and I argue for the latter. The argument has four parts. I define a model-free pair of quantities, time saved and excess escaped defects, and one decision number built from them: the break-even defect cost. I give a baseline model of checking in which, until the model is good enough not to check, a calibrated worker's escaped-defect rate does not depend on how good the model is; checking time grows with the logarithm of the model's error rate; and over-trust buys minutes on the clock at a larger cost in total minutes. I show what randomized studies can and cannot identify, and I replicate METR's developer study from its public data: the same 246 issues support a slowdown of 19% or 24% depending on the estimand (34% without adjusting for a chance imbalance), and AI helped on somewhere between 3% and 82% of them. Finally, I restate the published record, with stated bounds in place of imported point assumptions. In GDPval's own review-every-output workflow, review time alone caps GPT-5's speedup at 3.6x; the consultants outside the frontier were faster, and more often wrong. Last, I test two of the model's predictions in a small pre-specified experiment on AI reviewers checking AI drafts of the same kind of brief, built from SEC filings made after the models' training data were collected. Told the drafter's true error rate, the reviewer checked more as it rose, but all-or-nothing, switching near where its prompt's costs break even. Told that the drafter erred ten times less often than it did, it checked 7 fewer of 9 figures and let a wrong figure through in 22.5% of briefs instead of none: faster and worse, entirely through reduced effort.

Enhancing Deep Learning Model Performance through Data Generation Strategies: Classification of Olive Tree Diseases

arXiv:2610.35984v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Deep learning models are highly effective in a wide range of domains, particu larly in plant disease diagnosis. However, their performance is often limited by challenges such as insufficient data and class imbalance. In our work, we propose a comprehensive data generation strategy to overcome these limitations. First, we apply various data generation techniques, including traditional augmentation methods, CutMix, MixUp, and synthetic image generation using Deep Convolu tional Generative Adversarial Networks (DCGANs), to create new data samples. Second, we exploit this generated data in three scenarios: (S1) balancing class distribution in the original dataset, (S2) increasing the number of samples per class, and (S3) expanding the dataset while maintaining balanced classes. Third, we evaluate the impact of these data distributions (S1, S2, S3) on model perfor mance using the EfficientNetV2B0 architecture with pre-trained and fine-tuned weights. The experimental results show a substantial improvement in classifica tion accuracy, emphasizing the critical role of data diversity and class balance. These findings highlight the effectiveness and adaptability of the proposed strat egy of data generation and distribution for enhancing dataset quality, offering promising applications in plant disease detection and precision agriculture.

The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents

arXiv:2610.36077v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multimodal intelligence can be viewed as human-guided co-evolution between worlds and agents through interaction. However, scaling this process faces two bottlenecks: constructing heterogeneous, verifiable environments is costly, and manual Harness iteration cannot keep pace with growing task diversity and complexity. A key insight is that tasks with different modalities and surface forms can share the same underlying capability requirements. We therefore introduce The Trinity, a recursive self-improvement framework coupling world generation with Harness evolution through three roles: Creator, Explorer, and Evolver. Drawing on verified execution trajectories, Evolver shifts the basis of task organization from surface categories to shared requirements, reorganizing the original task tree into a hierarchical capability tree. We introduce Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real: for heterogeneous tasks at the same node, Creator abstracts their common requirements into simplified, verifiable simulated worlds for capability learning, while evaluation on original tasks measures transfer. Along this capability tree, Explorer progressively evolves reusable capability modules through selective inheritance, forming a Harness tree whose validated updates accumulate across iterations within a fixed topology. The resulting Harnesses specialize in different capabilities, motivating a Capability-Aware Harness Router that uses public task information to select compatible module configurations from the tree. Our evolved Harnesses improve mean performance by 34 points across 28 training benchmarks and 26 points across 18 unseen benchmarks, including two real-world embodied tasks.

CohortFlow: A Steerable Clinical Cohort Curation Agent with Localized Semantic Correction

arXiv:2610.36100v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Language model agents for clinical cohort curation should accommodate evolving eligibility criteria without repeatedly processing unchanged requirements. We introduce CohortFlow, a steerable agent that represents natural language corrections as localized updates to a structured cohort specification and reconciles them with a persistent execution graph. Explicitly recorded semantic dependencies enable affected computations to be selectively invalidated while preserving valid prior results. On 100 scenarios derived from clinical-trial eligibility criteria, comprising 126 correction turns, localized steering achieves 77.0% requirement micro-F1 versus 76.4% for full regeneration using the same 8B model, a small numerical advantage that is not statistically significant ($p=0.493$). It reduces mean correction latency by 64.8% after completion and 44.3% during execution, with 38.1% fewer model calls. In a separate proof-of-concept evaluation over MIMIC, all 13 cohort states execute successfully, while correction trajectories demonstrate selective preservation of unaffected computations and recomputation of changed or dependent nodes. Together, these results show that localized semantic steering can substantially reduce correction latency and model calls while maintaining strong requirement-level accuracy.

Evolving to Match Capabilities: Improving Harness Self-Evolution via Skill Adaptation

arXiv:2610.33024v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Harness self-evolution offers a practical route for LLM agents to improve from execution experience without updating model parameters. Its effectiveness depends on whether models can successfully act on the guidance provided by their harnesses. However, we find that harness benefits are not uniform across model capabilities: skills that improve stronger models can provide little benefit or even degrade performance for weaker ones. Across seven models and three benchmarks, we identify 103 task-skill pairs where skills improve GPT-5.6-luna by 31.1 points but degrade Gemma-4-E4B by 8.8 points. In 86% of failed weak-model runs, the violated requirement is already stated in the skill. This motivates skill adaptation: reformulating existing skills to match the executor's capabilities and make their guidance easier to execute. To achieve this, we diagnose failures in tool execution, context management, instruction following, and verification, and distill these diagnoses into a fixed meta-skill that guides adaptation during harness self-evolution. Guided by the meta-skill, a capable external rewriter, Kimi-K3, adapts skills without execution evidence, improving the three weak executors by 5.1-14.1 points over the original skills. We then incorporate this adaptation into a five-round self-evolution loop, where each model revises its own skill from execution trajectories and verifier feedback. Across seven executors, from Gemma-4-E4B to Kimi-K2.5, the meta-skill consistently improves final scores by 2.0-5.1 points over the same loop without the meta-skill. Our findings provide practical design guidance for developing more effective harness self-evolution methods.

Can Language Models Learn While They Think? Testing Gradient Adaptation Across Architectures

arXiv:2610.33025v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Letting a language model take gradient steps on the text it is reading, known as dynamic evaluation, lowers its loss on the rest of the document. Its behaviour on hybrid linear-attention architectures, on diffusion language models, and on standard long-context benchmarks has not been measured. We measure it. With one SGD step per 128-token chunk on the MLP output projections of the last quarter of blocks, no training, and a 2k-token window, softmax-attention models (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, Gemma-4-E4B) lose 5-8% of their next-token loss over a document and outperform the same frozen model given the full 32k-token context. Hybrid Gated-DeltaNet models (Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.8-27B) gain under 1% at every learning rate: the linear-attention blocks already perform the adaptation. The mechanism transfers to masked-diffusion models with a larger effect, 8% on Nemotron-Diffusion-8B and 17-20% on DiffusionGemma-26B, once the rate is lowered and updates are clipped. Across RULER, BABILong, LongBench-v2, SCOTUS, HotpotQA, MuSiQue, ContractNLI and citation completion, at 100 or more items on two model sizes, weights updated on the full document recover none of the accuracy lost to context truncation: the adaptation is style and vocabulary, not retrieval. Writing facts with the next-token loss stores the prompt format rather than the fact (30% recall under chat prompting against 80% in context) and costs 36-38 GSM8K points at every dose. Writing a conclusion as behaviour instead, by distilling the model's own conclusion-conditioned judgments into a low-rank adapter, reaches the in-context ceiling (0.83 and 0.88 against 0.84 and 0.88 on 100 games) with GSM8K unchanged, and the conclusion can come from an inquiry loop that found it by asking information-gain questions.

Lessons from Luck: Do LLM-Written Lessons Carry Chance into an Agent's Next Decision?

arXiv:2610.33029v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Self-improving agents write lessons from the outcomes of their runs; when outcomes are noisy, a lesson can carry chance into later decisions. We measure one link of this chain in a registered study. In 256 synthetic worlds, each a recurring task with two interchangeable tools of recorded reliability, a scripted previous run used the more reliable tool (a good decision) or the less reliable one (a bad decision), with an assigned outcome: success or a transient failure. A model wrote a lesson from the run with ReasoningBank's memory-writing instruction; a fresh session of the same model, holding the authoritative log and the previous result, chose the next run's first tool with one memory item: that lesson, the lesson from the other outcome, an irrelevant item, or an unreasoned directive. The protocol was frozen, deposited and timestamped before any confirmatory call; 11 LLM endpoints gave 90,084 answers. After a chance failure of the better tool, the panel's lessons carried the chance (registered reading luck-carrying; interaction +0.112, 95% interval [+0.097, +0.126]): readers named the worse tool in 11.6% of next runs with the lesson written from that failure, against 0.4% with an irrelevant item and 0.4% with the lesson written from a success. The effect was +0.042 where the log stated that failures are independent and +0.181 where it did not (descriptive). After a lucky success of the worse tool, the interaction was bounded below 0.10 (+0.002), a bound, not evidence of no harm. In exploratory readings, 6 endpoints carried the chance (up to +0.352); 5 were bounded below 0.10. In secondary comparisons, a three-sentence decision-quality note in the writing instruction lowered the worse-tool rate after a chance failure to 7.5%, and one instruction for both outcomes with a no-change option to 3.1%; neither met the registered harm-reduction rule at every such endpoint.

Feature Scaling Beats Architecture Choice in Offline Respiratory Screening for Low-Resource Clinics

arXiv:2610.33030v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Deep models are the usual choice for respiratory sound classification, but in clinics without GPUs or connectivity that choice carries a cost. We compare classical and deep classifiers on a five-class respiratory-sound corpus of 1,211 clips (asthma, COPD, pneumonia, Bronchial, healthy) from 201 patient groups, with a 3.86:1 class imbalance, using pre-registered patient-grouped cross-validation (25 runs per configuration). An SVM over 30 summary features (MFCC, ZCR and RMS statistics) reaches macro recall 0.78 and 0.71 recall on the rarest class. A 1.13M-parameter convolutional recurrent network over log-Mel spectrograms reaches 0.75 to 0.77, and no difference between model families is resolved. The largest single effect we measure is a preprocessing effect rather than an architectural one. Standardizing the SVM's features, a routine step for RBF kernels, moves its macro recall from 0.51 to 0.78, a 27-point swing wider than any gap between model families here. Augmentation did not help: SMOTE, waveform augmentation and SpecAugment have no measurable effect. Latency does not separate the models (51 to 74 ms per clip on a CPU-only laptop), but the classical models avoid a 449 MB PyTorch runtime. Clip-level splits, which let a patient's recordings fall on both sides, inflate macro recall by 7.5 to 9.8 points. Recording source is strongly tied to label (Cramer's V = 0.65 between native sampling rate and class), and in an exploratory analysis clips recorded at a class's minority rate are mostly assigned to classes typical of that rate; we state this confound as the paper's main limitation.

Machine learning enabled design of sustainable alloys considering real world constraints

arXiv:2610.35946v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Designing sustainable alloys must reconcile high performance with real-world constraints, including feedstock scarcity and variability, processing and energy requirements, regulatory thresholds, and economic limitations. Addressing these constraints reaches far beyond today's conventional performance-focused frameworks. It needs to consider not only complex property-processing-performance trade-offs but also sustainable production pathways. Here, we propose a physics-informed ensemble (PIE) learning framework that integrates fundamental structure-property relationships with processing routes and sustainability criteria for mechanism-based alloy design. It predicts properties from composition-microstructure relationships and infers processing routes under sustainability constraints, establishing an interdisciplinary approach to identifying sustainable alloy solutions. We validate the approach for Mg alloys, a lightweight yet energy-intensive metallic material class, considering performance, cost, and carbon emissions. Trained on a benchmark dataset, the PIE model systematically explores plausible alloy compositions and microstructures to construct a digital library of millions of candidate alloys. From this space, we identify two sustainable Mg alloy solutions that overcome the conventional performance-cost-emission trade-off: a rare-earth-containing alloy increases yield and tensile strength by 72.8 percent and 61.1 percent, respectively, over alloys from the same family at comparable cost and emissions; and a rare-earth-free alloy reduces cost and carbon emissions by 40.4 percent and 88.9 percent, respectively, relative to its primary-feedstock counterpart, without compromising mechanical performance. This work introduces a general, physics-informed, and quantifiable framework for designing sustainable alloys under real-world constraints.

Amorphization-Depth-Dependent Bismuth Redistribution and Activation in Silicon for Quantum Devices

arXiv:2610.35993v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Near-surface bismuth donors are promising for silicon quantum devices because of their rich electron-nuclear spin structure and long spin coherence, but their electrical activation is strongly influenced by implantation-induced amorphization. We compare redistribution and electrical activation of 20 keV Bi implanted directly into Si and after Si preamorphization. The two routes produce amorphous-layer depths of approximately 47 nm and 177 +/- 15 nm, respectively. After annealing at 650 C for 300 s, Rutherford backscattering spectrometry and channeling reveal comparable substitutional Bi fractions of 91 +/- 2% and 89 +/- 4%, whereas Hall measurements indicate Hall-derived electrical activation yields of approximately 60% and 90%, respectively. Depth-resolved ion-scattering analysis reveals a buried Bi-related feature at or beyond the initial amorphous/crystalline interface, within the end-of-range region, together with increasing surface segregation at higher annealing temperatures. The buried feature is consistent with trapping near residual end-of-range defects. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy and low-temperature photoluminescence on the Bi-only sample reveal no resolvable Bi-oxide contribution and a donor-related optical signature, respectively. These results show that amorphization depth and regrowth geometry govern Bi redistribution and influence electrical activation beyond substitutional incorporation alone.

STEM-Bank-26: An Open Dataset of Analytical Scanning Transmission Electron Microscopy Images and Spectra

arXiv:2610.36086v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) is a versatile, multi-modal method for studying materials at the nanoscale. It integrates multiple signal sources to provide atomic resolution imaging alongside spectroscopy for elemental composition and electronic structure. Despite its utility, few datasets spanning multiple years of data have been published. We introduce STEM-Bank-26, a 929 GB open dataset of 37,921 STEM images that spans 1,095 material samples across 1,499 sessions and includes energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) or electron-energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) data for 65.5% of sessions. A Hybrid Brownian Dynamics-kinetic Monte Carlo Framework for Coupled Magnetic and Rotational Dynamics of Nanoparticles

arXiv:2610.36102v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Simulating the coupled magnetic and rotational dynamics of suspended magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) over experimentally relevant timescales remains computationally demanding, particularly under low-frequency alternating magnetic fields. Here, we develop a hybrid Brownian Dynamics--kinetic Monte Carlo (BD--kMC) framework that combines thermally activated magnetic switching with particle rotation while retaining local fluctuations of the magnetic moment. These fluctuations are incorporated through Metropolis Monte Carlo (MC) sampling, allowing the moment to depart from the energy minima and thereby capturing the thermally averaged anisotropy torque that governs particle rotation. To preserve the distinction between local fluctuations and magnetic reversals, a barrier-trapping criterion confines MC trial moves to the current energy well, leaving thermally activated switching to the kMC process. For non-interacting single-domain MNPs, comparison with stochastic Landau--Lifshitz--Gilbert (LLG) simulations demonstrates agreement in the equilibrium torque and stationary easy-axis orientation, including the field-driven transition from predominantly perpendicular to parallel alignment. The resulting BD--kMC framework provides an efficient approach to coupled magnetic and Brownian Dynamics, reducing the computational cost of exploring the long timescales relevant to low-frequency magnetic applications.

Magnons in {\alpha}-Fe2O3 microdisks: from thermal to coherent excitation

arXiv:2610.37205v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Hematite is a prototypical antiferromagnetic insulator for spintronic and magnonic applications, yet controlled studies of coherent magnons in thin films remain challenging due to strain sensitivity and spatial inhomogeneity. Here, we investigate magnon excitations in lithographically patterned hematite microdisks, and obtain reproducible magnon spectra from multiple individual microdisks. Brillouin light scattering reveals bulk-like field-dependent thermal magnon resonances with an ultralow effective damping parameter on the order of ~10-5. Coherent magnons are further excited using a stripline antenna adjacent to the microdisk, revealing a power-dependent frequency blue shift accompanied by a nonlinear evolution of the coherent response under strong microwave driving. Field-dependent analysis indicates a power-dependent increase of the effective magnetic anisotropy field, suggesting a tunable nonlinear response of antiferromagnetic magnons in laterally confined microdisks. These results establish patterned hematite thin films as a platform for exploring coherent and nonlinear antiferromagnetic magnon dynamics.
